这一时期的宣教事业，很少表现出奥古斯丁 (Augustine)、哥伦巴 (Columba) 和卜尼法斯 (Boniface) 那个伟大宣教时代的热忱，成就就更少了。其原因在于支配中世纪教会的种种野心，以及欧洲面临的外来威胁。十字军以征服圣地取代了使非基督教民族归信的事业。教皇权力力图对西方基督教世界一切世俗事务取得最高控制权，这也占满了教会的视野。这两项运动几乎耗尽了教会的宗教力量。另一方面，蒙古人，或称鞑靼人 (Tartars)，如晚间的豺狼一般凶猛，从中亚蜂拥而出，使整个欧洲陷入恐惧；十三世纪最受关注的大事之一，就是阻止他们向欧洲大陆中部推进。法国南部的异端宗派威胁着教会的统一，也分去了部分注意力，而这些注意力原本可能用于使异教徒归信的努力。

两种新的力量出现了，即商人与殖民者；他们在这一世纪的作用，相当于现代商业的船舶、火车，以及非洲和其他国家地理探险家的工作。在波罗的海沿岸的一些时候，以及在亚洲，商人和探险家走在宣教士前面，或与他们沿着同样的路线前进。在使德国东北部的蛮族部落臣服于基督教世界规范的努力中，刀剑与殖民所起的作用，不亚于属灵的手段。

这一时代的宣教史分为三篇：在德国东北部和波罗的海沿岸直至里加 (Riga) 的异教民族中间，在北非的穆罕默德教徒中间，以及在中亚和东亚的蒙古人中间。姓名流传至今的主要宣教士，有在欧洲东北部劳作的班贝格的奥托 (Otto of Bamberg) 和维策林 (Vicelinus)，遍行亚洲的鲁布鲁克 (Rubruquis) 和孟高维诺的约翰 (John of Monte Corvino)，以及在非洲传道的阿西西的方济各和雷蒙德·卢勒。

犹太人在教会手中所受的待遇，也理当在此论述。

§ 74. 德国东北部的宣教

这一时期开始时，源于斯拉夫族的文德人 (Wends)1，是波罗的海沿岸从吕贝克 (Lübeck) 至里加各省的主体人口；在如今的波美拉尼亚 (Pommerania)、勃兰登堡 (Brandenburg) 和萨克森 (Saxony) 部分地区，还杂居着一些既非日耳曼人也非斯拉夫人、而是立陶宛人的族群。2查理曼 (Charlemagne) 不曾试图征服易北河 (Elbe) 以东的地方。毗邻这些部落领地的维尔茨堡 (Würzburg)、美因茨 (Mainz)、哈尔伯施塔特 (Halberstadt)、费尔登 (Verden) 和不来梅—汉堡 (Bremen-Hamburg) 各主教区，为使他们归信，所做的工作甚少，或全无作为。在奥托一世 (Otto I.) 统治下，哈弗尔贝格 (Havelberg)、迈森 (Meissen)、梅泽堡 (Merseburg) 等教区相继设立，以推进这一工作。在967年的拉文纳 (Ravenna) 会议上，奥托过早地夸称文德人已经归信。

在文德人历史单调平庸的背景上，唯一显得突出的人物是戈特沙尔克 (Gottschalk)；他在英格兰归信，并将若干部落联合成一个广大的帝国。他关心本族人民的归信，在梅克伦堡 (Mecklenburg)、吕贝克、奥尔登堡 (Oldenburg) 等中心兴建了教堂和修道院。但随着戈特沙尔克于1066年遇害，国土四分五裂，此后文德各部便成为波兰和萨克森诸公爵征服的对象。使他们基督教化的尝试遭到了激烈抵抗。文德人与日耳曼人彼此仇恨。3这些蛮族部落实行多妻制、杀婴，4焚烧死者的尸体，并有自己的圣泉、墓地和偶像。

用了两个世纪，才将这些民族所占据、而今大部分由日耳曼人居住的地区置于教会的支配之下。所用的手段包括宣教士的教导、条顿骑士团 (Teutonic Knights) 所挥舞的刀剑，以及由日耳曼殖民者移居这些土地。教会的圣礼与仪式被放在首位，作为与教会联合的条件。同时，也坚决要求废除野蛮习俗。主教区和修道院成为新近接受福音地区的属灵堡垒。

最早怀着真正宣教热忱在文德人中间劳作的，是西班牙熙笃会士伯纳德 (Bernard)。他完全不懂当地语言；他的赤足和粗陋修道服，也不大有助于他接近这个祭司衣着华美的民族。

继伯纳德之后的是班贝格主教奥托，任期为1102–1139年；他应伯纳德的请求，首次前往巡行。他赢得了“波美拉尼亚使徒”的称号。1124年，他带着霍诺留二世 (Honorius II.) 的祝福，并有充足的教士助手，启程前往该地。他赢得了波美拉尼亚公爵弗拉提斯拉夫 (Wratislaw) 的好感；这位公爵年轻时曾作为战俘受洗。在皮里茨 (Pyritz) 有七千人受洗的事，因与当时实行的浸礼有关，格外值得注意。水池凿入地下，池沿高出地面及膝。据编年史家记载，5人很容易走入池中。每个池上都搭起帐篷遮盖。奥托向民众讲授七件圣礼，6并坚决要求他们放弃多妻制和杀婴。

在斯德丁 (Stettin)，他摧毁了特里格拉尔 (Triglar) 神的庙宇，并将神像的三重头颅送往罗马，作为十字架得胜的标志。

1128年，奥托第二次巡行波美拉尼亚。他借助译员讲话。他的教导之后，便是庙宇的拆毁和教堂的兴建。他既关心人民属灵的福祉，也关心他们物质的生活，并将葡萄引入该地。7他的工作由马格德堡的诺伯特 (Norbert of Magdeburg) 和普雷蒙特雷会士 (Premonstrants) 继续进行。

维策林卒于1154年，是文德人宣教史上另一位最重要的人物；他在如今的荷尔斯泰因 (Holstein) 及邻近地区传道。他曾在巴黎求学三年，后受不来梅—汉堡大主教阿达尔伯特 (Adalbert) 委派，从事这项工作。北萨克森的熊爵阿尔伯特 (Albert the Bear)，1133–1170年，以及狮子亨利 (Henry the Lion)，1142–1163年，对当地瓦格里人 (Wagrians) 和阿博特里特人 (Abotrites) 部落发动的残酷战争，并不有助于为基督教预备道路。维策林创办了重要的塞格贝格 (Segeberg) 修道院，后来成为培养宣教士的中心。吕贝克接受了基督教，1148年，维策林被按立为奥尔登堡主教。

德国宣教士的足迹远达里加。在征服当地部落的过程中，刀剑起了显著作用。在教皇的批准下，十字军征伐接踵而至。利沃尼亚人 (Livonians) 最初是通过迈因哈德 (Meinhard，卒于1196年)8认识基督教的；他曾在塞格贝格受训。在他之前，不来梅商人已经到达那里，他本人也是乘坐不来梅商船启程宣教的。他被按立为新设的于克斯屈尔 (Uexkull) 教区主教；1202年，该教区改名为里加教区。

迈因哈德的继任者、熙笃会士贝尔托尔德 (Berthold)，起初试图以教导和慈善事业打开道路，却遭暴力驱逐。1198年，他率领塞莱斯廷 (Coelestin) 下令组织的十字军返回。他战死沙场之后，其继任者阿佩尔德恩的阿尔伯特主教 (Albert of Apeldern) 于1199年率领另一支军队进入该地。随后，这些土地向殖民者开放。在英诺森三世 (Innocent III.) 的批准下，阿尔伯特创立了宝剑弟兄会 (Brothers of the Sword)。他们的征战为教会进入爱斯托尼亚 (Esthaonia) 和塞内加伦 (Senegallen) 开辟了道路。1224年，多尔帕特 (Dorpat) 主教座设立，后来的多尔帕特大学便以此地为名。

1207年，德国修道院院长戈特弗里德 (Gottfried) 来到魏克塞尔河 (Weichsel) 沿岸的东普鲁士 (Eastern Prussia)。两位当地首领经由来自波美拉尼亚的修士克里斯蒂安 (Christian) 的传道而归信，将自己的土地献给教会，并前往罗马，在那里受洗。克里斯蒂安于1212至1215年间被任命为普鲁士主教。1228年，条顿骑士团 (Teutonic Knights) 大团长萨尔察的赫尔曼 (Hermann of Salza) 接受邀请，协助使当地各部落归信。1217年，霍诺里乌斯三世 (Honorius III.) 曾下令发动十字军征伐；1230年，格列高利九世 (Gregory IX.) 又重申此令。条顿骑士团既获许诺，将分得被征服土地中的很大一部分，自然十分乐意以刀剑推进宗教扩张。从1230年至1283年，他们连年征战，并通过兴建设防城镇来巩固自己的地位，如1231年建立的库尔姆 (Kulm) 和托恩 (Thorn)，以及1255年建立的柯尼斯堡 (Königsberg)。他们征服之处，德国殖民者便接踵而至。1243年，英诺森四世 (Innocent IV.) 将普鲁士分为四个主教区：库尔姆、波梅萨尼亚 (Pomesania)、萨梅兰 (Sameland) 和埃尔姆兰 (Ermeland)。按照安排，主教们将获得被征服领土的三分之一。1308年，德意志骑士团夺取了魏克塞尔河口的但泽 (Danzig)，次年将总部设于马林堡 (Marienburg)。9经过1410年的坦嫩贝格战役 (Tannenberg) 和1466年的《托恩和约》，他们失去了魏克塞尔河以西的普鲁士领土，此后其领地仅限于东普鲁士。当大团长勃兰登堡的阿尔布雷希特 (Albrecht of Brandenburg) 接受宗教改革，并使公国成为其家族的世袭领地时，这一骑士团的历史便告结束。

§ 75. 在穆罕默德教徒中的宣教

在穆罕默德教徒中的宣教，与两个重要的名字相联系：阿西西的方济各 (Francis of Assisi) 和雷蒙德·卢勒 (Raymundus Lullus)。他们的工作并未产生任何持久的成果，而这一题目的内容也尽于他们的事迹。十字军征伐只会加深基督徒与穆罕默德教徒之间的鸿沟，使这位假先知的追随者更加掩耳不听基督教使者的呼吁。

1213年，方济各会修士前往摩洛哥 (Morocco)，领受了殉道者的冠冕，却未对穆罕默德教徒产生任何影响。101219年，圣方济各偕同十一位同伴前往叙利亚和埃及。有关记载既简略，又不确实。11方济各在阿克 (Acre) 登岸，随后来到达米埃塔 (Damietta) 城墙下的十字军营地；据记载，他曾在苏丹面前并向穆罕默德教军队传道。相传，苏丹深受方济各讲道的感动，准许方济各会修士进入圣墓，无须纳贡。

雷蒙德·卢勒，1235年？—1315年，毕生致力于使穆罕默德教徒归信，并以殉道之死印证了自己的热忱。他是中世纪西南欧所产生的最卓越人物之一。他三次前往非洲宣教，并首倡在大学设立教席，教授东方语言，训练宣教士。他还撰写了许多论著，旨在使不信者确信基督教的真理。

卢勒出生于马略卡岛 (Majorca) 的帕尔马 (Palma)。他的父亲曾协助从撒拉森人 (Saracens) 手中夺取巴利阿里群岛 (Balearic islands)，因而获得殊荣。卢勒本人娶妻生子，却在宫廷过着享乐放荡的生活，将诗才用于创作情欲的十四行诗。三十一岁时，他追逐自己所迷恋的一名女子，直追到一座教堂里；那女子突然袒露胸部，显出癌症病灶，这景象使他止住了狂放的脚步。他前往孔波斯特拉 (Campostella) 朝圣，随后退居故乡岛上的兰达山 (Mt. Randa)。他在此隐居五年，并于1272年加入圣方济各第三会。他开始关注使穆罕默德教徒及其他异教徒归信的工作，向一位由他赎身、脱离奴隶身份的摩尔人 (Moor) 学习阿拉伯语。他获得了一个知识体系的启示，称之为“普遍科学”，即 ars magna（大术）或 ars generalis（通用之术）。1276年，在阿拉贡王 (king of Aragon) 的帮助下，他在马略卡岛创办了一所由方济各会管理的学院，以阿拉伯语和叙利亚语训练宣教士。

卢勒前往巴黎求学，并发展他的普遍科学。后来，他又回到那里讲学。1286年，他前往罗马，力陈自己的宣教计划，却未能赢得教皇的赞许。1292年，他从热那亚 (Genoa) 启航，踏上前往非洲的宣教旅程。在突尼斯 (Tunis)，他试图与穆罕默德教学者展开公开辩论，却未成功。骚乱随之而起，卢勒险些丧命。返回欧洲后，他再次设法争取教皇的支持，仍然徒劳无功。1309年，他第二次乘船前往突尼斯，再次寻求与穆罕默德教徒辩论。有人许诺，只要他改信穆罕默德教，便给他种种荣誉；他回答说：“我也应许你们，若你们回转，相信耶稣基督，就必得丰盛的财宝和永生。”

卢勒再次被暴力逐出非洲后，于1311年向克雷芒五世 (Clement V.) 和维埃纳会议 (council of Vienne) 陈述自己的计划。他在会上提出对阿威罗伊 (Averrhoes) 哲学的驳论，并力促设立东方语言教席。会议下令在阿维尼翁 (Avignon)、巴黎、牛津、萨拉曼卡 (Salamanca) 和博洛尼亚 (Bologna) 设立此类教席，教授希腊语、希伯来语、迦勒底语和阿拉伯语。12

这位不知疲倦的宣教士虽已年近八十，仍再次启程前往突尼斯。他在布吉亚 (Bougia) 的传道如同从前一样引发骚乱；卢勒被拖到城外，遭人用石头打。他被抛下时已是半死，后由基督徒水手救起，送上船去，最终死于海上。他的遗骨保存在帕尔马。

在将近五十年的时间里，这位非凡的人物一直倡议采取种种措施，将福音传给穆罕默德教徒。据我们所知，他的讲道和护教著作并未对不信者——无论犹太人还是穆罕默德教徒——产生影响；但他的名字将永远与设立宣教学院这一新观念相联系，使那些有志献身宣教事业的人得以在其中接受外语训练。然而，卢勒不仅是一位热情洋溢的宣教倡导者，还是一位诗人和精通经院哲学的思想家。13西班牙未曾产生过另一位如此著名的经院学者。他著述甚丰；在将思想运用于自然科学方面，人们曾将他与同属方济各会的罗杰·培根 (Roger Bacon) 相提并论。14

他将自己的普遍科学应用于医学与法律、占星术与地理学、语法与修辞，也用于解决神学问题。15它是一把开启一切思想领域的钥匙，无论天上还是地下。他以字母表中的字母代表观念，将这些字母置于圆圈及其他数学图形之中。通过转动圆圈、移动线条，这些观念便形成各种关系，从而展现出一个真理体系。例如，“上帝”一词便由此与九个字母 B-K 相联系，分别代表九种属性：良善、伟大、永恒、能力、智慧、意志、美德、真理和荣耀。或者，B-K 这些字母代表九个问题，如什么，quid；由什么，de quo；为什么，quare；多少，quantum。将这些问题应用于上帝，便可得出有效的界定，如“上帝的存在是必然的”。卢勒这种万花筒式的方法，很可能取自犹太人和阿拉伯人的资料，而他本人也称之为卡巴拉式 (Cabalistic) 方法。

卢勒的哲学吸引了一批追随者，称为卢勒派 (Lullists)。瓦伦西亚 (Valencia) 和阿拉贡的大学都曾传授他的哲学。焦尔达诺·布鲁诺 (Giordano Bruno) 也曾从中汲取思想。宗教裁判官艾梅里库斯 (Eymericus) 成为卢勒派的死敌，向罗马教廷控告其领袖的教导，并出示格列高利十一世 (Gregory XI.) 于1372年颁布的诏书，谴责这些教导为异端。16腓力二世 (Philip II.) 读过这位马略卡人的一些著作，并将附有批注的书本留在埃斯科里亚尔图书馆 (Escurial library)。卢勒的著作被列入1559年保罗四世 (Paul IV.) 的《禁书目录》(Index)，但特伦托会议 (council of Trent) 下令将其除名。1619年，一项教皇裁决以危险为由，禁止卢勒的学说。1847年，庇护九世 (Pius IX.) 批准在马略卡使用纪念“圣雷蒙德·卢勒”的日课；在那里，他被视为圣徒。自利奥十世 (Leo X.) 以来，方济各会一直在其《日课经》(Breviary) 中纪念这位西班牙人。

§ 76. 在蒙古人中的宣教

十二世纪的西欧对中亚和如今中华帝国的了解，几乎如同斯皮克 (Speke)、利文斯通 (Livingstone) 和斯坦利 (Stanley) 旅行之前人们对中非湖区的了解一样稀少。向中亚和东亚传播福音的任务，自然落在了聂斯托利派 (Nestorians) 身上，他们在埃德萨 (Edessa) 和尼西比斯 (Nisibis) 都设有学校。他们的足迹远达中国，但九世纪以后，其学校衰落，随之进入一段停滞时期。个别聂斯托利派信徒在亚洲宫廷担任顾问或医生，身居有影响力的职位；聂斯托利派妇女也成为蒙古首领的母亲。然而，没有一个亚洲部落接受他们的信仰。

十二世纪，一种绚丽的幻想传遍欧洲，说中亚存在着一个强大的基督教神权国家，由约翰长老 (Presbyter John)，通常称为祭司王约翰 (Prester-John)，统治。17关于这个据说兼具君王与祭司身份的神秘人物，各种极其离奇的传闻四处流传。据弗赖辛的奥托 (Otto of Freisingen) 记载，1145年，迦巴拉 (Gabala) 的某位主教曾向尤金三世 (Eugenius III.) 报告，说此人是一位聂斯托利派基督徒，是东方三博士之一的后裔，并曾在一场大战中击败穆罕默德教徒。18一封据称出自这位统治者、致君士坦丁堡皇帝曼努埃尔 (Manuel) 的书信说，约翰接受七十位国王的朝贡，其臣民中有以色列的十个支派；他每日设宴款待十二位大主教和二十位主教，他的国度流奶与蜜。19后来，传说他的领土逐渐扩展到阿比西尼亚 (Abyssinia) 和印度。

与这位奇异人物建立联系，并使他臣服于罗马，是几位教皇认真关注的事情。1177年，亚历山大三世 (Alexander III.) 派遣自己的医生腓力 (Philip)，命他向这位国王介绍西方基督教世界的信仰。他还致函称这位国王为“我在基督里最亲爱的儿子、印度诸地之王、祭司中最圣洁的约翰”。随着人们认真寻访这个王国，这种幻想便逐渐消退。鲁布鲁克 (Rubruquis) 从据说约翰曾统治的地区写信回欧洲，说那里几乎找不到知道祭司王约翰的人，流传的故事也大为夸张。他又说，一位名叫科尔汗 (Coirchan) 的统治者之后，由一位名叫约翰的聂斯托利派牧人继位。奥佩特 (Oppert) 推测，“Coirchan”一词经由叙利亚语的 Juchanan，到了欧洲便被称为约翰。一位同名的君主，中国人称为图留塔沙 (Tuliu Tasha)，从中国向西逃亡，在中亚建立了一个王国。他的臣民中有聂斯托利派信徒。中国的传统说，这位君主是佛教徒。于是，这个用吉本 (Gibbon) 的话说“长久以来投欧洲之轻信所好”的传说，便如此逐渐消散了。

十二和十三世纪，亚洲见证了庞大蒙古帝国的建立。在未开化的民族中，军事天才几乎从未有过比帝国的创建者成吉思汗 (Zenghis Khan) 及其继承者，尤其是忽必烈 (Kublai) 和蒙哥 (Mangu)，更为惊人的表现。20帝国疆域从中国海延伸至第聂伯河 (Dnieper)，从巴格达 (Bagdad) 直抵北极。他们的军队令欧洲胆寒。人们担心，穆罕默德教徒在西班牙所成就的，蒙古人将在整个欧洲大陆重演。他们摧毁了莫斯科，并于1241年推进至波兰的克拉科夫 (Cracow) 和匈牙利的布达佩斯 (Buda Pesth)。帝国迅速瓦解，分为四大部分：包括中国和西藏的大汗帝国；中亚帝国；延伸至高加索 (Caucasus) 的波斯；以及在俄罗斯和西伯利亚组织松散的金帐汗国 (Golden Horde)。211245年第一次里昂会议 (Council of Lyons) 的目的之一，就是筹划抵御这些被称为鞑靼人 (Tartars) 的迫在眉睫的威胁；22而在1274年的第二次里昂会议上，一个由十六名鞑靼人组成的使团出席，希望结盟对抗撒拉森人 (Saracens)。

教会向这些部族派出了几个传教使团，其中一些人获大汗宫廷接见。他们当中最无畏、最富冒险精神的是威廉·鲁布鲁克 (William Rubruquis，或 Ruysbroeck)，他堪称那个时代的利文斯通，并以生动的笔触记述了自己的经历。孟高维诺的约翰 (John of Monte Corvino) 则远行至北京，当时欧洲人称其为坎巴卢 (Cambaluc)，蒙古人称其为汗八里 (Khanbaligh)，意即“汗之城”。

蒙古人作战虽残酷无情，在宗教上却颇为宽容。这部分是因为他们中间没有任何明确成形的崇拜体系。蒙哥汗回应鲁布鲁克的劝说时说：“我们蒙古人相信只有一位上帝，我们在他里面生，也在他里面死。但正如上帝给一只手造了不同的手指，他也给人不同的道路通向自己。他给你们基督徒的是圣经，给我们的则是占卜者和卜筮者。”

忽必烈对马可·波罗 (Marco Polo) 的话，也表现了同样的精神：“地上有四个不同的族群，各自敬拜一位先知。基督徒以基督为他们的神，撒拉森人以穆罕默德为神，犹太人以摩西为神，异教徒则以索戈蒙巴尔汗 (Sogomombar-Khan，即佛陀) 为神。我尊重并敬奉这四位，并祈求他们中间至高的那一位赐我帮助。”亚历山大·塞维鲁 (Alexander Severus) 将亚伯拉罕、基督、俄耳甫斯 (Orpheus) 和其他异教神祇的塑像并列在一起时，或许也并未高明多少。直到传教士与蒙古人接触之后，东方诸汗才接受佛教，而波斯和西方的各部族则选择了伊斯兰教的礼仪。

1245年，英诺森四世 (Innocent IV.) 派遣四名多明我会士前往波斯的蒙古首领那里，又派三名方济各会士前往大汗本人那里。下一次尝试出自路易九世 (Louis IX.)，当时他正从事自己的第一次十字军远征。鞑靼地区的蒙古首领派遣使者，在塞浦路斯 (Cyprus) 拜访了这位法国国王。23路易投桃报李，于1248年派回两名多明我会士，两年后又派出两名方济各会士；他还怀着看见鞑靼人归信的虔诚盼望，送去一顶绣有圣经场景的帐幕，其构造使它搭起时形如一座小礼拜堂。我们关于蒙古人的最早可靠资料，正是来自这两名方济各会士中的鲁布鲁克。他发现，聂斯托利派祭司使用自己并不懂得的叙利亚语礼仪，并与穆罕默德教徒和佛教徒一道，为大汗的酒杯祝福。鲁布鲁克抵达哈拉和林 (Karkorum)，在蒙哥汗的宫廷受到热情接待。蒙哥的一位书记是基督徒，另一位是穆罕默德教徒，第三位是佛教徒。大汗面前举行了一场宗教辩论。鲁布鲁克宣称上帝的一切诫命都包含在圣经之中后，有人问他，他是否认为蒙哥遵守了这些诫命。这位传教士巧妙地答道：“他的愿望是将上帝的一切诫命陈明于大汗面前，那时大汗便能自行判断，自己是否遵守了这些诫命。”

波斯的蒙古首领与基督徒都是埃及哈里发的敌人；蒙古人征服巴格达哈里发国之后，教皇派遣使团前往波斯，多明我会和方济各会也在当地建立了修院；但蒙古人在十四世纪接受伊斯兰教之后，便迫害基督徒，修院也遭到摧毁。

在中亚的察合台蒙古人 (Jagatai Mongols) 中间，事态也循着同样的轨迹发展。起初，在1340年，传教士获准开展工作。马里尼奥拉的约翰 (John of Marignola) 传道，并为归信者施洗。后来，这些蒙古人也接受了穆罕默德教，并迫害基督徒。

在蒙古人统治的中华帝国，传教工作显出更有望结实的前景。尼科洛·波罗 (Nicolo Polo) 和马费伊·波罗 (Maffei Polo)24向格列高利十世 (Gregory X.) 转达了忽必烈汗的请求，请他派遣传教士，将基督教和欧洲习俗教导给自己的人民。波罗一行归途中，有两名多明我会士同行，马可·波罗也在队伍之中。这些传教士并未抵达目的地。三年后，又派出了方济各会士。尼古拉四世 (Nicholas IV.) 派遣的方济各会士孟高维诺的约翰抵达了坎巴卢的大汗宫廷；1303年，来自科隆 (Cologne) 的方济各会士阿诺德 (Arnold) 也前来与他会合。他们将新约和诗篇译成鞑靼语，买来并训练了一百五十名男孩，建造了两座教堂，其中一座紧邻宫殿，且比宫殿更高，并为六千名归信者施洗。1307年，约翰被任命为北京大主教，archiepiscopus Cambalensis，于1330年去世。诸汗转而信奉佛教，而于1368年兴起掌权的明朝则废除了基督教。直到三百年后，才由耶稣会士在中国重新开展宣教工作。

但愿我们能够说，中世纪教会对于犹太人——按肉体说是亚伯拉罕的子孙——之福祉的关切，有它对收复这些人古老故土上的圣地所表现的关切的十分之一。然而，我们不能这样说。虽然各处偶有教皇、主教和君王倾向于本着人道精神对待他们，但欧洲占支配地位的情绪却是仇恨与鄙视。那些正倾尽国力派出军队以重新夺回圣墓的民族，也一同参与了迫害犹太人的行动。

犹太人自身的行为，多少也提供了一些解释。他们成功而往往不择手段的金钱交易、对财富的炫耀、在社会交往中排斥外人的倾向、种族上的傲慢，以及行事隐秘，都使基督徒公众的容忍达到了极限。25教会会议的法令和民事敕令使人无法合理怀疑，他们曾以冒犯的方式，蔑视基督教信仰的礼仪与象征。他们造成的激愤固然很大，但这并不足以为从波希米亚 (Bohemia) 到大西洋各地犹太人所遭受的、毫无基本人道的待遇辩护。人们为使他们归信而作的积极努力，似乎表露出的教会傲慢精神，丝毫不亚于基督徒的仁爱精神。可敬者彼得在其致犹太人论著的序言中说：“在整个古代世界，唯有你们并非不认识基督；然而，万民都已聆听，唯有你们不听。各方言都已承认他，唯有你们否认。别人看见他、听见他、领会他，唯有你们仍然眼瞎、耳聋、心如顽石。”

迫害犹太人的理由有三项：1. 他们的祖先将基督钉在十字架上，而这个被预定要承担此举之罪责与刑罚的种族，正在领受其应得的报应；2. 他们对基督徒儿童犯下骇人的暴行，并亵渎圣饼和十字架；3. 他们索取高额利息，欺压基督徒。在任何基督教国家，他们都得不到安全。他们在各国都是外人，在任何一国都不享有公民权。“基督的仇敌”和“背信者”是对他们的常见称呼，教会法学家和神学家也使用后一种说法。受难日的礼仪中包含这样的话：“让我们也为背信的犹太人祈祷。”26格拉提安 (Gratian) 的《教令集》、第三次和第四次拉特兰会议 (Lateran)，以及其他会议，都将犹太人与撒拉森人归在同一条教规之下。27即使像可敬者彼得这样杰出的人物，对撒拉森人的好评也比对犹太人更多。

考察犹太人所受的待遇，必须顾及三类人：教皇及高级教士、君王，以及民众和他们的祭司。

逐一考察各位教皇，他们的言论总体上反对不人道的措施，并且一贯反对强迫犹太人受洗。大格列高利 (Gregory the Great) 保护他们，使其免受意大利南部狂热的迫害。1247年，英诺森四世否认了对他们谋杀儿童的指控，并以绝罚警告那些压迫他们的基督徒。281419年，马丁四世 (Martin IV.) 颁布诏书，宣称自己遵循前任教皇，命令不得干扰他们在会堂中的礼拜，不得强迫他们接受洗礼，也不得因他们与基督徒之间的商业交易而迫害他们。另一方面，英诺森三世 (Innocent III.) 的榜样却助长了最严厉的措施，而尤金四世 (Eugenius IV.) 很快便废除了其前任马丁四世的禁令。

至于君王，犹太人被视为处于他们的特殊管辖之下。他们任意向犹太人征税、没收其财产，并将他们逐出国境。将犹太人保留下来作为财源，符合君王的利益；因此，他们倾向于保护犹太人，使其免遭民众盲目偏见与愤怒所引发的暴力。腓特烈二世 (Frederick II.) 使犹太人永为奴仆，以此报复他们将基督钉在十字架上的罪行。29

十字军运动兴起之初，便伴随着针对犹太人的暴力爆发。1216年，英诺森三世为迫害他们确立了持久的法律依据。1492年他们被逐出西班牙，标志着中世纪犹太人苦难这出戏剧的高潮。英格兰、德意志、法国、西班牙、葡萄牙和匈牙利都参与了对他们的迫害。他们在意大利所受的苦难最少。数以万计的人被烧死，或以其他方式处死。他们在不同时期几乎被逐出过每一个国家。他们常被迫在受洗与死亡之间作出选择。宁愿受死的人数，可能多于接受洗礼的人数。不过，接受洗礼的人，大多后来又公开回归祖先的信仰，或秘密遵行其仪式。30

一个值得注意的事实是，在这几个世纪的迫害中，犹太人，尤其是西班牙和法国的犹太人，开展了蓬勃的著述活动。革尔顺 (Gerschom)、拉施 (Raschi) 和金希家族 (Kimchis) 都属于法国。迈蒙尼德 (Maimonides) 和图德拉的本雅明 (Benjamin of Tudela)，位列西班牙众多犹太学者之首。格雷茨 (Graetz) 的著作中，满载着医学及其他学术领域杰出学者的姓名与成就。31

教会与基督教国家很早就走上了反犹主义的道路。中世纪的立法紧随早期法令的先例。32306年的埃尔维拉会议 (Elvira) 禁止基督徒与犹太人同席吃饭及通婚。439年，狄奥多西二世 (Theodosius II.) 禁止他们担任公职。强令他们在受洗与死亡之间作出选择的世俗敕令，始于西班牙国王西西布特 (Sisibut)。当君王保护犹太商人时，如在里昂 (Lyons) 所发生的那样，高级教士便激烈抗议；里昂大主教阿戈巴德 (Agobard) 就是如此，尽管他在某些方面堪称现代开明思想的先驱。33这一时期的法令包括：禁止犹太人雇用基督徒乳母、仆人或劳工，禁止他们公开售肉，在星期日或节期工作，聘用基督徒医生，34或放债取息；又命令他们在复活节向祭司缴纳一笔钱，并在衣服上佩戴一块标识布或其他物件。另一方面，基督徒被禁止参加犹太人的葬礼和婚礼，并因向犹太人借款而受罚。

所有规定中，最具羞辱性的，莫过于要求犹太人穿着特殊服装，或在衣服上佩戴标识布。规定这块布须置于胸前，或胸前背后都置一块，使人远远就能认出佩戴者，正如古时麻风病人因呼喊“不洁净”而为人识别；也是为了防止基督徒在不知情的情况下，与这些受鄙视的人发生肉体关系。在斯蒂芬·兰顿 (Stephen Langton) 的倡议下，1222年的牛津会议 (Oxford) 规定佩戴羊毛布标；1275年，爱德华一世 (Edward I.) 命令所有七岁以上的犹太人佩戴黄色布标。路易九世 (Louis IX.) 规定布标应为红色或藏红花色，英格兰国王则规定为黄色。其大小与形状都有详尽的法令规定。第四次拉特兰会议以其崇高权威支持这项服饰规定，并下令各地一律施行。格雷茨博士断言，这项法令是使其同胞蒙受羞辱的最沉重一击。他宣称，英诺森三世加给犹太人的苦难，比此前他们所有仇敌所加的还多，并指责他是第一位将教会不人道的严酷手段施于犹太人的教皇。35

英诺森所持的立场是：上帝有意将犹太人保存下来，使他们像杀人者该隐一样在地上流离；他们因自身的罪而注定为奴，直到末后的日子、他们归信的时候来到。36

神学家们赞同这种看法。早于英诺森半个世纪的可敬者彼得 (Peter the Venerable)，与这位伟大的教皇从同一角度看待此事，并对犹太人发出了可怕的谴责。他在致法国路易七世 (Louis VII.) 的信中疾呼：“在遥远的地方与十字架的仇敌作战，却让那些亵渎基督、比撒拉森人邪恶得多的犹太人逍遥法外，不受惩罚，这有什么益处呢？犹太人比撒拉森人更应受咒诅和憎恨；因为后者承认基督由童女所生，犹太人却否认，并且亵渎这项教义和基督教的一切奥秘。上帝不要将他们彻底灭绝，而要像保存杀弟兄的该隐一样保存他们，使他们遭受更严厉的折磨与羞辱。自基督受难以来，上帝至为公义的严厉就是如此对待犹太人的，并且将继续如此对待他们，直到世界的末了，因为他们是受咒诅的，也配受咒诅。”37他劝人夺去他们的不义之财，并将没收所得的钱财用于从撒拉森人手中夺回圣地。

伯尔纳 (Bernard) 的想法却不同。在筹备第二次十字军东征时，修士拉杜尔夫 (Radulf) 沿莱茵河往来，煽动民众反对犹太人；克莱尔沃 (Clairvaux) 的这位修道院长便起来反对这个“蛊惑民众者”——尼安德 (Neander) 如此称呼拉杜尔夫。38他给美因茨 (Mainz) 大主教写了一封措辞激切的书信，提醒他，主恩待那以善报恶的人。他疾呼道：“教会日日说服犹太人，使他们归信，岂不比一次用刀剑将他们杀尽，更能彻底胜过他们吗！”这种偏见是何等深重，从以下事实便可看出：当伯尔纳与拉杜尔夫当面对质时，他必须凭借自己圣洁的全部声望，才能平息美因茨的骚动。39

转看英格兰，我们发现纽伯格的威廉 (William of Newburgh)、罗杰·德·霍维登 (Roger de Hoveden) 及其他编年史家都赞同迫害犹太人。迪韦齐斯的理查 (Richard of Devizes)40谈到“将犹太人献给他们的父魔鬼为祭”，并将“这些吸血者连同鲜血一并送入地狱”。马修·帕里斯 (Matthew Paris) 在某些记述中，似乎并不完全赞同民众的仇视。

在英格兰杰出的教会人士中，犹太人至少有两位友善的代言人：林肯的休 (Hugh of Lincoln) 和罗伯特·格罗斯泰斯特 (Robert Grosseteste)。格罗斯泰斯特提出了不可灭绝犹太人的原则，理由是律法曾借着他们赐下，而且他们在经历第二次被掳之后，最终将照《罗马书》第十一章所言，接受基督教。不过，他宣称，正如亚伯是基督的预表，该隐也是犹太人的预表。为上帝怜悯的缘故，应当保存他们，使基督得荣耀；但为上帝公义的缘故，君王应将他们置于奴役之下，使他们应验关于该隐的预言，在地上漂泊流离。应当以强制手段阻止他们从事放债取息的行业。41这位主教的信是写给温彻斯特伯爵遗孀 (dowager countess of Winchester) 的；她曾在自己的领地上，收容被西蒙·德·孟福尔 (Simon de Montfort) 逐出莱斯特 (Leicester) 的犹太人。另一封同样写于1244年的信，证明他并未全然超越其时代的偏见；他在信中要求各位会吏长阻止犹太人与基督徒毗邻而居。格罗斯泰斯特的前任林肯的休，在1190年犹太人遭抢掠屠杀时曾保护他们；犹太人也出席他的葬礼，以表敬意。42

无论多么严重的罪名，都可以加在犹太人头上。1348年黑死病横扫欧洲时，没有人想到把撒拉森人视为这场瘟疫的祸首。有罪的是犹太人。骇人的谣言说，他们在法国南部和西班牙配制毒药，大批运出，用以污染泉水。从巴塞罗那 (Barcelona)、塞维利亚 (Seville)，直到瑞士和德意志的各个城市，这个不幸的民族都因这项被指控的罪行而遭受迫害。1349年，在斯特拉斯堡 (Strassburg)，全城两千名犹太居民悉数被捕；凡不肯受洗的，都在他们自己的墓地中被烧死，财产也被没收。在埃尔福特 (Erfurt) 及其他地方，所有犹太居民都因焚杀或驱逐而被清除。

教会法禁止取息的规定，使人轻易便能找到借口，宣告所欠犹太人的债务不具约束力。取息为特土良 (Tertullian) 和居普良 (Cyprian) 所谴责，起初无论平信徒或教士都被禁止取息，埃尔维拉会议的规定就是如此；但在325年的尼西亚会议 (Nice) 上，这项禁令仅限于教士。后来，耶柔米 (Jerome)、奥古斯丁 (Augustine) 和利奥一世 (Leo I.) 又将禁令适用于所有基督徒。格拉提安将其收入教会法。很少有其他议题像取息问题一样，受到中世纪各次教会会议如此普遍的关注。431179年，亚历山大三世 (Alexander III.) 在第三次拉特兰会议上甚至宣称，取息既为《新约》所禁止，也为《旧约》所禁止。克莱门特五世 (Clement V.) 则在1311年的维埃纳会议 (Vienne) 上，将这类立法推至顶点：他宣布，一切准许取息的国家及城市法律均属无效，并宣告否认取息为罪就是异端。利率高低并无区别。一切利息都是高利贷。教会法典既有这样的法令，竟还有借款人认为自己有义务偿还欠犹太人的债务，这才令人惊讶。

尤金三世 (Eugenius III.) 许诺，凡参加第二次十字军东征的人，均可免付欠犹太债主的利息。格列高利九世 (Gregory IX.) 向后来的十字军作出了同样的许诺。

犹太人屡次被指控为举行宗教仪式而谋杀基督徒儿童，尤其是在逾越节期间。这种几乎令人难以置信的罪行，一再激起基督徒民众的狂热，致使犹太民族蒙受其所经历的一些最惨重的苦难。44

在法国，腓力·奥古斯都 (Philip Augustus) 于1182年以所谓一名基督徒儿童被钉十字架为借口，将犹太人逐出国境，并没收其财产。路易九世在他最后一次十字军东征出发的前一年，美男子腓力 (Philip the Fair) 在1306年和1311年，以及其他法国君主，都曾重颁驱逐令，但执行起来始终不如西班牙严格。路易九世还下令销毁《塔尔古姆》(Targum) 的所有抄本。1239年，格列高利九世致函法国、卡斯蒂利亚 (Castile)、阿拉贡 (Aragon)、葡萄牙和英格兰的各位大主教，下达了同样的命令。45

在德意志，自第一次十字军东征以来，犹太人不断遭受暴力袭击，但通常得到皇帝的保护，免遭民众狂怒的侵害。十五世纪时，他们于1432年被逐出萨克森 (Saxony)，1435年被逐出施派尔 (Spires) 和苏黎世 (Zürich)，1438年被逐出美因茨，也被逐出其他地方。

在英格兰，伦敦 (London)、林肯 (Lincoln)、牛津 (Oxford) 以及另外三四座城市中所谓的犹太人聚居区 (Jewries)，各有特殊的法庭和组织形式，国内通常的法院与之毫无关系。46 从亨利二世 (Henry II.) 在位时期（1133–1189年）起，英格兰犹太人生活的详细记载开始出现；当时，主教、神父和修道院都乐于向犹太人借贷。九所熙笃会 (Cistercian) 修道院抵押给了著名的林肯的亚伦 (Aaron of Lincoln)，此人于1187年去世。他夸口说，圣奥尔本斯 (St. Albans) 是用他的钱建成的；弗里曼 (Freeman) 以此夸言为证，说明犹太人的傲慢令人难以容忍。彼得伯勒 (Peterboro) 的圣奥斯瓦尔德 (St. Oswald) 的一只手臂，也曾作为抵押品扣在一个犹太人手中。通常的利息是每镑每周收取二便士，即年息百分之四十三；有时甚至高达百分之八十。威森登 (Wissenden) 的牧师赫伯特 (Herbert) 向林肯的亚伦借款120马克、每周付息二便士的借据，至今尚存。47 国王任意向犹太人征收特别税。他们如同森林一样，属于国王。48 约翰 (John) 和亨利三世 (Henry III.) 统治下，征敛之频繁、数额之苛重，是众所周知的。1210年征税时，许多人离开了这个王国。前文提到的布里斯托尔 (Bristol) 那个犹太人的著名事件，正发生在此时：约翰命人每天拔掉他一颗牙，直到他向王室国库缴纳一万马克为止。马修·帕里斯 (Matthew Paris) 的记述极其引人入胜；但直到四个世纪以后，另一位历史学家托马斯·富勒 (Thomas Fuller) 在评论这桩中世纪的牙科手术时，才竟敢说，这个犹太人“若早些屈服，便可保住牙齿；若再顽抗久些，便可省下钱财；如今这一番交涉，却使他的钱袋和牙床都空了。我们在此当谴责人的残酷，并赞叹上天的公义；因为世上的君王向犹太人勒索的这一切钱财，不过是他们向上帝偿付所拖欠的债款；这笔债他们永远无法偿清，那就是将基督钉十字架。”旧有的偏见实在难以消亡。

亨利三世的征敛变得如此令人无法忍受，以致1255年犹太人恳求准许他们离开国境。若再依据马修·帕里斯的记载，国王拒绝了这一请求，随后便像“另一个提多 (Titus) 或维斯帕先 (Vespasian)”一样，把他们包给了自己富有的弟弟、康沃尔伯爵理查 (Richard, Earl of Cornwall)，好叫“他自己既已剥了他们的皮，理查便可再挖去他们的内脏”。49

英国十字军启程参加第三次十字军东征时，肆意劫掠犹太人。据编年史家记述，他们愤愤不平的是，犹太人竟丰衣足食，尚有余裕，而他们这些急于踏上前往耶路撒冷的漫长旅途的人，却连最基本的需要也无法满足。50 正是在这个时期，理查一世 (Richard I.) 加冕礼当晚，发生了那场不分男女老幼的可怕屠杀。在约克 (York)，五百人被困在城堡中；男人们绝望之下，先杀死自己的妻子和女儿，随后其中许多人也被烧死。51

有关基督徒男童被钉十字架的指控，使英格兰各地民众的情绪激动到了可悲的地步。其中较为著名的案件，有1144年诺里奇的威廉 (William of Norwich)、1168年格洛斯特的哈罗德 (Harold of Gloucester)、1181年埃德蒙兹伯里的罗伯特 (Robert of Edmonsbury)，以及1255年林肯的休 (Hugh of Lincoln)。这些儿童虽然在民间被称为圣徒，却无一获教会正式封圣。马修·帕里斯所记述的、据称施加在林肯的休身上的暴行，骇人听闻，不宜逐一详述。这位编年史家还插言说，这种事“据说经常发生”。围绕小休事件的激愤之中，十八个犹太人被处以绞刑。52 令人惊异的是，人们竟相信这一骇人的指控，而当时反对这种信念的抗议之声，竟无一传到我们这里。

一些英格兰犹太人在恐惧的压力下接受了洗礼，也有一些出于自愿。据我所知，后一类最早的事例见于安瑟伦 (Anselm) 的记载。53 那位归信者后来成为修士。各地也偶有基督徒改信犹太教的个别事例。一位执事曾因这种罪行被处以绞刑。54

中世纪英格兰犹太人历史的最后一幕，是1290年爱德华一世 (Edward I.) 将他们驱逐出境。从那时起直到查理时代 (Caroline age)，英格兰都没有犹太居民。克伦威尔 (Cromwell) 在伦敦给予他们保护，也为自己增添了声誉。

犹太人在西班牙受到的待遇，被公正地视为这个民族在中世纪所遭遇的最残酷的待遇。爱德华一世驱逐一万六千名犹太人离开英格兰时，尚且保护他们免遭抢掠。但西班牙的斐迪南 (Ferdinand) 颁布严酷的敕令，命令境内犹太臣民离开西班牙时，却似乎袖手旁观，毫无怜悯之意。西班牙通过其教会会议，一向是限制性立法的先行者。宗教裁判所 (Inquisition) 的设立，使这个民族的生活日益艰难，尽管起初那些无情的规条并不适用于他们。迫害使全国充斥着并非真心归信的人，即 conversos；这些人便成了宗教裁判法庭管辖的对象。

斐迪南和伊莎贝拉 (Isabella) 在1492年施下了最后一击；这一年也是新世界被发现之年，而在新世界的一部分地区，日后将实行世上前所未有的宗教宽容。这道敕令将所有未受洗的犹太人逐出西班牙。其背后的动机是宗教性的，执行者也是宗教人士。直接的导火索，是所谓拉瓜尔迪亚 (La Guardia) 的儿童被钉十字架一事所引起的恐慌；这个孩子被称为“拉瓜尔迪亚的圣婴”——el santo niño de la Guardia——这是指控犹太人谋杀儿童的案件中最臭名昭著的几宗之一。55 洛佩·德·维加 (Lope de Vega) 及其他西班牙作家的作品，使此案在西班牙文学中广为人知。据略伦特 (Llorente) 记载，斐迪南受犹太使团和西班牙显贵的恳求所感动，正准备修改判令；这时，托尔克马达 (Torquemada) 急忙来到国王和王后面前，举起苦像，高声说道：“加略人犹大用三十块银钱卖了基督。两位陛下如今却要用三千杜卡特卖他。他就在这里，拿去卖吧。”

据估计，1492年夏天离开西班牙的犹太人有170,000至400,000人。56 他们前往意大利、摩洛哥和东方；另有100,000人应曼努埃尔王 (Manuel) 的邀请，进入葡萄牙。但他们在那里的逗留注定十分短暂。1495年，一道敕令又将那个老选择摆在他们面前：受洗或死亡；十四岁以下的儿童被强行从父母身边带走，接受基督教神圣的洗礼。十年之后，两千名被指并非真心归信的人遭到了冷血屠杀。

这就是中世纪西欧犹太人被迫经历的苦难之剧。与这种待遇相对照，人们又曾作过什么努力，以福音的感召来赢得犹太人呢？然而，我们大可以反问：当如此迫害的精神盛行时，还能指望任何劝说赢得他们吗？爱与这样的敌意，怎能并行不悖？试图说服他们的努力，主要借助论著和辩论。安瑟伦论救赎的著作《神为何成为人》(cur deus homo) 虽非针对犹太人而写，但他却说，自己的论证足以说服犹太人和异教徒。格罗斯泰斯特 (Grosseteste) 在1231年所写的《论律法礼仪的止息》(de cessatione legalium) 中，力求说明旧律法已经应验，并证明基督的神性。57 这些论著中最著名的一部，出自可敬者彼得 (Peter the Venerable) 之手。在米涅 (Migne) 的版本中，它占了一百四十多栏，若按现代普通开本排印，将成为一部三百多页的书。其标题很难赢得所针对之人的好感，题为《驳犹太人积久的顽梗》(inveteratam duritiem)。作者继而根据希伯来圣经证明基督的神性，同时宣称：“对于瞎眼的人，连光也如同黑夜，太阳也如同幽暗的阴影。”

彼得引证了许多经文，以证明基督是上帝的儿子；从其中一处，便可略窥他某些论证的性质。《以赛亚书》六十六章9节说：“耶和华说：我既使胎儿临产，岂不使他生出来呢？你的上帝说：我既使人生育，岂能使母胎闭塞呢？”作者接着说：“犹太人哪，要证明上帝之子的出生，还有什么比这更清楚呢？因为上帝若生子，就其生子而言，他必然是父；而上帝之子，就其受生而言，也必然是子。”在着手证明弥赛亚已经降临时，彼得天真地说：“如果论证完毕之后，犹太人竟还胆敢以为自己活着，那么彼得手中拿着歌利亚的刀，站在犹太人伏倒的身躯之上，就要用这兵器毁灭他，‘用刀刃’把他那亵渎的头颅劈成两半。”58

倘若温厚的克吕尼 (Cluny) 院长、可敬者彼得，尚且以这样傲慢的语气对待犹太人，那么，对于撰写《论犹太人的背信》(Perfidy of the Jews) 的布卢瓦的彼得 (Peter of Blois) 等其他作者，又能有何指望呢？

西南欧曾采用公开辩论的办法。不少犹太人——用格雷茨 (Graetz) 的话说，就是“学者、医生、作家和诗人”59——出于确信而接受了基督教信仰，并且“热衷于劝人归信，仿佛他们生来就是多明我会士”。在公开辩论中，由拉比代表与选出的基督教论辩家交锋。归信的犹太人往往代表基督教一方。其中最著名的托尔托萨 (Tortosa) 辩论，历时一年九个月，跨1413–1414年，共举行六十八次会议。据报，这场论辩实力的较量之后，许多人接受了洗礼。本笃十三世 (Benedict XIII.) 以诏书公布了自己的结论，认为强迫受洗违反教会法规，因而予以禁止；但他坚持犹太人须佩戴特殊的布片标记，并规定他们每年须听三次基督教讲道，分别在复活节、将临期和仲夏。拉蒙·卢尔 (Raymundus Lullus) 为使犹太人归信，呼吁设立希伯来语教席；多明我会士佩尼亚福特的雷蒙德 (Raymundus of Peñaforte) 也提出了同样的呼吁。十五世纪初，雄辩的传道者文森特·费雷尔 (Vincent Ferrer) 的传教活动取得了成功；即使按最低的估计，受他影响而受洗的人也有两万。西班牙归信者中最杰出的是拉比所罗门·赫莱维 (Solomon Helevi，1353–1435年)，他曾任布尔戈斯 (Burgos) 大主教。基督教学者库萨的尼古拉 (Nicolas of Cusa)，即便不是生来就是犹太人，也有犹太血统。

在伦敦，曾有人试图通过一种类似大学社会服务区的机构来接触犹太人，这就是旨在保护犹太归信者的“归信者之家”(domus conversorum)。它于1233年设立，王室国库答应每年拨款七百马克，供其维持；但关于它所发挥作用的报告，却没有流传下来。

诚然，这些努力使这幅阴暗的图景略有缓和，但也仅仅是一点而已。犹太人的民族排他性，以及基督教世界认为他们一旦发达便表现出来的桀骜傲气，至今仍使人难以通过阐述基督教的论据来打动他们。归信者确实是有的。尼安德 (Neander) 生来是犹太人。保卢斯·卡塞尔 (Paulus Cassel) 和阿道夫·萨菲尔 (Adolf Saphir) 也是如此。德利奇 (Delitzsch) 的父母中有一位是犹太人。据德林格 (Döllinger) 的说法，三十年前柏林已有两千名犹太血统的基督徒。幸而今日至少在西方教会中，已不再有人认为，教会应当协助天意，为基督被钉十字架施行报复；而这种思想在中世纪曾支配基督徒的心灵。即使没有别的理由，单从中世纪教会的经验来看，现代教会所应采取的对待方式，也当是本着弟兄般的信任和基督徒之爱的精神。

The missionary operations of this period display little of the zeal of the great missionary age of Augustine, Columba, and Boniface, and less of achievement. The explanation is to be found in the ambitions which controlled the mediaeval church and in the dangers by which Europe was threatened from without. In the conquest of sacred localities, the Crusades offered a substitute for the conversion of non-Christian peoples. The effort of the papacy to gain supreme control over all mundane affairs in Western Christendom, also filled the eye of the Church. These two movements almost drained her religious energies to the full. On the other hand the Mongols, or Tartars, breaking forth from Central Asia with the fierceness of evening wolves, filled all Europe with dread, and one of the chief concerns of the thirteenth century was to check their advance into the central part of the continent. The heretical sects in Southern France threatened the unity of the Church and also demanded a share of attention which might otherwise have been given to efforts for the conversion of the heathen.

Two new agencies come into view, the commercial trader and the colonist, corresponding in this century to the ships and trains of modern commerce and the labors of the geographical explorer in Africa and other countries. Along the shores of the Baltic, at times, and in Asia the tradesman and the explorer went in advance of the missionary or along the same routes. And in the effort to subdue the barbarous tribes of Northeastern Germany to the rules of Christendom, the sword and colonization played as large a part as spiritual measures.

The missionary history of the age has three chapters, among the pagan peoples of Northeastern Germany and along the Baltic as far as Riga, among the Mohammedans of Northern Africa, and among the Mongols in Central and Eastern Asia. The chief missionaries whose names have survived are Otto of Bamberg and Vicelinus who labored in Northeastern Europe, Rubruquis, and John of Monte Corvino who travelled through Asia, Francis d’Assisi and Raymundus Lullus who preached in Africa.

The treatment which the Jews received at the hand of the Church also properly belongs here.

§ 74. Missions in Northeastern Germany.

At the beginning of this period the Wends,(1) who were of Slavic origin, were the ruling population in the provinces along the Baltic from Lübeck to Riga with elements in the territory now covered by Pommerania, Brandenburg intermingled, and parts of Saxony, which were neither German nor Slavic but Lithuanian.(2) Charlemagne did not attempt conquest beyond the river Elbe. The bishoprics of Würzburg, Mainz, Halberstadt, Verden, and Bremen-Hamburg, bordering on the territories of these tribes, had done little or nothing for, their conversion. Under Otto I. Havelberg, Meissen, Merseburg, and other dioceses were established to prosecute this work. At the synod of Ravenna, 967, Otto made the premature boast that the Wends had been converted.

The only personality that looms out above the monotonous level of Wendish history is Gottschalk, who was converted in England and bound together a number of tribes in an extensive empire. He was interested in the conversion of his people, and churches and convents were built at Mecklenburg, Lübeck, Oldenburg, and other centres. But with Gottschalk’s murder, in 1066, the realm fell to pieces and the Wend tribes from that time on became the object of conquest to the dukes of Poland and Saxony. Attempts to Christianize them were met with violent resistance. Wends and Germans hated one another.(3) These barbarous tribes practised polygamy, infanticide,(4) burned the bodies of their dead, had their sacred springs, graves, and idols.

Two centuries were required to bring the territories occupied by these peoples, and now for the most part inhabited by Germans, under the sway of the Church. The measures employed were the instructions of the missionary, the sword as wielded by the Teutonic Knights, and the colonization of the lands with German colonists. The sacraments and ritual of the Church were put in the forefront as conditions of union with the Church. The abolition of barbarous customs was also insisted upon. The bishopric and the convent were made the spiritual citadels of the newly evangelized districts.

The first to labor among the Wends, who was actuated by true missionary zeal, was the Spanish Cistercian, Bernard. He was without any knowledge of the language and his bare feet and rude monastic garb were little adapted to give him an entrance to the people whose priests were well clad.

Bernard was followed by Otto, bishop of Bamberg, 1102–1139, who made his first tour at Bernard’s instance. He won the title of Apostle of Pommerania. In 1124 he set his face towards the country, furnished with the blessing of Honorius II. and well supplied with clerical helpers. He won the goodwill of the Pommeranian duke, Wratislaw, who, in his youth, as a prisoner of war, had received baptism. The baptism of seven thousand at Pyritz has a special interest from its hearing on the practice of immersion followed at that time. Tanks were sunk into the earth, the rims rising knee high above the ground. Into these, as the chronicler reports,(5) it was easy to descend. Tent-coverings were drawn over each of them. Otto instructed the people in the seven sacraments(6) and insisted upon the abandonment of polygamy and infanticide.

At Stettin he destroyed the temple of the god Triglar, and sent the triple head of the idol to Rome as a sign of the triumph of the cross.

In 1128 Otto made a second tour to Pommerania. He spoke through an interpreter. His instructions were followed by the destruction of temples and the erection of churches. He showed his interest in the material as well as spiritual well-being of the people and introduced the vine into the country.(7) His work was continued by Norbert of Magdeburg and the Premonstrants.

Vicelinus, d. 1154, the next most important name in the history of missions among the Wends, preached in the territory now covered by Holstein and the adjoining districts. He had spent three years in study at Paris and was commissioned to his work by Adalbert, archbishop of Bremen-Hamburg. The fierce wars of Albert the Bear, of North Saxony, 1133–1170, and Henry the Lion, 1142–1163, against the Wagrians and Abotrites, the native tribes, were little adapted to prepare the way for Christianity. Vicelinus founded the important convent of Segeberg which became a centre of training for missionaries. Lübeck accepted Christianity, and in 1148 Vicelinus was ordained bishop of Oldenburg.

The German missionaries went as far as Riga. The sword played a prominent part in the reduction of the local tribes. Under papal sanction, crusade followed crusade. The Livonians received their first knowledge of Christianity through Meinhard, d. 1196,(8) who had been trained at Segeberg. He had been preceded by Bremen merchants and set forth on his mission in a Bremen merchant vessel. He was ordained bishop of the new diocese of Uexkull whose name was changed in 1202 to the diocese of Riga.

Meinhard’s successor, the Cistercian Berthold, sought at first to win his way by instruction and works of charity, but was driven away by violence. He returned in 1198, at the head of a crusade which Coelestin had ordered. After his death on the field of battle his successor, bishop Albert of Apeldern, entered the country in 1199 at the head of another army. The lands were then thrown open to colonists. With the sanction of Innocent III., Albert founded the order of the Brothers of the Sword. Their campaigns opened the way for the church in Esthaonia and Senegallen. In 1224 the see of Dorpat was erected, which has given its name to the university of Dorpat.

Eastern Prussia, lying along the Weichsel, was visited in 1207 by the German abbot, Gottfried. Two of the native princes were converted by Christian, a monk from Pommerania, donated their lands to the Church, and travelled to Rome, where they received baptism. Christian was made bishop of Prussia between 1212 and 1215. An invitation sent to the Teutonic Knights to aid in the conversion of the tribes was accepted by their grand-master, Hermann of Salza, in 1228. In 1217 Honorius III. had ordered a crusade, and in 1230 Gregory IX. renewed the order. The Teutonic Knights were ready enough to further religious encroachment by the sword, promised, as they were, a large share in the conquered lands. From 1230 to 1283 they carried on continual wars. They established themselves securely by building fortified towns such as Kulm and Thorn, 1231, and Königsberg, 1255. A stream of German colonists followed where they conquered. In 1243 Innocent IV. divided Prussia into four sees, Kulm, Pomesania, Sameland, and Ermeland. It was arranged that the bishops were to have one-third of the conquered territory. In 1308 the German Knights seized Danzig at the mouth of the Weichsel and a year later established their headquarters at Marienburg.(9) By the battle of Tannenberg, 1410, and the Peace of Thorn, 1466, they lost Prussia west of the Weichsel, and thereafter their possessions were confined to Eastern Prussia. The history of the order closed when the grand-master, Albrecht of Brandenburg, accepted the Reformation and made the duchy hereditary in his family.

§ 75. Missions among the Mohammedans.

Two important names are associated with the missions among the Mohammedans, Francis of Assisi and Raymundus Lullus, and with their labors, which were without any permanent results, the subject is exhausted. The Crusades were adapted to widen the gulf between the Christians and the Mohammedans, and to close more tightly the ear of the followers of the False Prophet to the appeals of the Christian emissary.

Franciscan friars went in 1213 to Morocco and received the martyr’s crown, but left no impression upon the Mohammedans.(10) St. Francis made his tour to Syria and Egypt in 1219, accompanied by eleven companions. The accounts are meagre and uncertain.(11) Francis landed at Acre and proceeded to the crusading camp under the walls of Damietta, where he is represented as preaching before the sultan and to the Mohammedan troops. The story is told that the sultan was so much touched by Francis’ preaching that he gave the Franciscan friars admission to the Holy Sepulchre, without payment of tribute.

Raymundus Lullus, 1235?-1315, devoted his life to the conversion of Mohammedans and attested his zeal by a martyr’s death. He was one of the most noteworthy figures produced during the Middle Ages in Southwestern Europe. He made three missionary tours to Africa and originated the scheme for establishing chairs at the universities to teach the Oriental languages and train missionaries. He also wrote many tracts with the aim of convincing unbelievers of the truth of Christianity.

Lullus was born in Palma on the island of Majorca. His father had gained distinction by helping to wrest the Balearic islands from the Saracens. The son married and had children, but led a gay and licentious life at court and devoted his poetic gifts to erotic sonnets. At the age of thirty-one he was arrested in his wild career by the sight of a cancer on the breast of a woman, one of the objects of his passion, whom he pursued into a church, and who suddenly exposed her disease. He made a pilgrimage to Campostella, and retired to Mt. Randa on his native island. Here he spent five years in seclusion, and in 1272 entered the third order of St. Francis. He became interested in the conversion of Mohammedans and other infidels and studied Arabic under a Moor whom he had redeemed from slavery. A system of knowledge was revealed to him which he called "the Universal Science," ars magna or ars generalis. With the aid of the king of Aragon he founded, in 1276 on Majorca, a college under the control of the Franciscans for the training of missionaries in the Arabic and Syriac tongues.

Lullus went to Paris to study and to develop his Universal Science. At a later period he returned and delivered lectures there. In 1286 he went to Rome to press his missionary plans, but failed to gain the pope’s favor. In 1292 he set sail on a missionary tour to Africa from Genoa. In Tunis he endeavored in vain to engage the Mohammedan scholars in a public disputation. A tumult arose and Lullus narrowly escaped with his life. Returning to Europe, he again sought to win the favor of the pope, but in vain. In 1309 he sailed the second time for Tunis, and again he sought to engage the Mohammedans in disputation. Offered honors if he would turn Mohammedan, he said, "And I promise you, if you will turn and believe on Jesus Christ, abundant riches and eternal life."

Again violently forced to leave Africa, Lullus laid his plans before Clement V. and the council of Vienne, 1311. Here he presented a refutation of the philosophy of Averrhoes and pressed the creation of academic chairs for the Oriental languages. Such chairs were ordered erected at Avignon, Paris, Oxford, Salamanca, and Bologna to teach Greek, Hebrew, Chaldee, and Arabic.(12)

Although nearly eighty years old the indefatigable missionary again set out for Tunis. His preaching at Bougia led, as before, to tumults, and Lullus was dragged outside of the city and stoned. Left half dead, he was rescued by Christian seamen, put on board a ship, and died at sea. His bones are preserved at Palma.

For a period of nearly fifty years this remarkable man had advocated measures for carrying the Gospel to the Mohammedans. No impression, so far as we know, was made by his preaching or by his apologetic writings upon unbelievers, Jew or Mohammedan, but with his name will always be associated the new idea of missionary institutes where men, proposing to dedicate themselves to a missionary career, might be trained in foreign languages. But Lullus was more than a glowing advocate of missions. He was a poet and an expert scholastic thinker.(13) Spain has produced no Schoolman so famous. He was a prolific author, and in his application of thought to the physical sciences, he has been compared to his fellow Franciscan, Roger Bacon.(14)

His Universal Science he applied to medicine and law, astrology and geography, grammar and rhetoric, as well as to the solution of theological problems.(15) It was a key to all the departments of thought, celestial and terrestrial. Ideas he represented by letters of the alphabet which were placed in circles and other mathematical diagrams. By the turning of the circles and shifting of lines these ideas fall into relations which display a system of truth. The word "God," for example, was thus brought into relation with nine letters, B-K, which represented nine qualities: goodness, greatness, eternity, power, wisdom, volition, virtue, truth, and glory. Or the letters B-K represented nine questions, such as, what, quid; from what, de quo; why, quare; how much, quantum. Being applied to God, they afford valid definitions, such as "God’s existence is a necessity." This kaleidoscopic method, it is not improbable, Lullus drew from Jewish and Arabic, sources, and he himself called it Cabalistic.

The philosophy of Lullus found a number of adherents who were called Lullists. It was taught at the universities of Valencia and Aragon. Giordano Bruno drew from it. Eymericus, the inquisitor, became the bitter foe of the Lullists, arraigned their leader’s teachings before the Roman court, and exhibited a bull of Gregory XI. (1372) condemning them as heretical.(16) Philip II. read some of the Majorcan’s writings and left annotated copies in the Escurial library. Lullus’ works were included in the Index of Paul IV., 1559, but ordered removed from the list by the council of Trent. A papal decision of 1619 forbade Lullus’ doctrine as dangerous. In 1847 Pius IX. approved an office for the "holy Raymundus Lullus" in Majorca, where he is looked upon as a saint. The Franciscans have, since the time of Leo X., commemorated the Spaniard’s memory in their Breviary.

§ 76. Missions among the Mongols.

Central Asia and what is now the Chinese Empire were almost as unknown to Western Europe in the twelfth century as the lake region of Central Africa was before the journeys of Speke, Livingstone, and Stanley. To the Nestorians, with their schools at Edessa and Nisibis, naturally belonged the task of spreading the Gospel in Central and Eastern Asia. They went as far as China, but after the ninth century their schools declined and a period of stagnation set in. Individual Nestorians reached positions of influence in Asiatic courts as councillors or physicians and Nestorian women became mothers of Mongol chiefs. But no Asiatic tribe adopted their creed.

In the twelfth century the brilliant delusion gained currency throughout Europe of the existence in Central Asia of a powerful Christian theocracy, ruled over by the Presbyter John, usually called Prester-John.(17) The wildest rumors were spread concerning this mysterious personage who was said to combine the offices of king and priest. According to Otto of Freisingen, a certain bishop of Gabala in 1145 had brought Eugenius III. the information that he was a Nestorian Christian, was descended from one of the three Wise Men, and had defeated the Mohammedans in a great battle.(18) A letter, purporting to come from this ruler and addressed to the Emperor Manuel of Constantinople, related that John received tribute from seventy kings, and had among his subjects the ten tribes of Israel, entertained at his table daily twelve archbishops and twenty bishops, and that his kingdom was overflowing with milk and honey.(19) Gradually his dominions were reported to extend to Abyssinia and India.

To put themselves into communication with this wonderful personage and bring him into subjection to Rome engaged the serious attention of several popes. Alexander III., 1177, sent his physician Philip with commission to inform the king of the faith of Western Christendom. He also addressed him in a letter as his "most dear son in Christ, John, king of the Indies and most holy of priests." The illusion abated as serious efforts to find the kingdom were made. Rubruquis wrote back to Europe from the region where John was reported to have ruled that few could be found who knew anything about Prester-John and that the stories which had been told were greatly exaggerated. He added that a certain ruler, Coirchan, had been followed by a Nestorian shepherd, called John. It has been conjectured by Oppert that the word "Coirchan," through the Syrian Juchanan, became known as John in Europe. A prince of that name whom the Chinese call Tuliu Tasha fled from China westwards, and established a kingdom in Central Asia. Nestorians were among his subjects. Chinese tradition has it that the prince was a Buddhist. Thus dwindles away a legend which, to use Gibbon’s language, "long amused the credulity of Europe."

In the twelfth and thirteenth centuries Asia witnessed the establishment of the vast Mongol empire. Scarcely ever has military genius among uncivilized peoples had more wonderful display than in its founders, Zenghis Khan and his successors, especially Kublai and Mangu.(20) The empire stretched from the Chinese Sea to the Dnieper, and from Bagdad to the Arctic. Their armies were the terror of Europe. What the Mohammedans had accomplished in Spain it was feared the Mongols would do for the whole continent. They destroyed Moscow and advanced as far as Cracow in Poland, and Buda Pesth in Hungary, 1241. The empire rapidly disintegrated, and was divided into four main sections: the empire of the Great Khan, including China and Thibet; the empire of Central Asia; Persia, extending to the Caucasus, and the loose kingdom of the Golden Horde in Russia and Siberia.(21) The first council of Lyons, in 1245, had as one of its objects to provide a defence against the imminent menace of these Tartars,(22) as they were called, and a delegation of sixteen of them appeared at the second council of Lyons, 1274, in the hope of forming an alliance against the Saracens.

The Church sent forth several deputations of missionaries to these tribes, some of whom were received at the court of the Great Khan. The most fearless and adventuresome of their number was William Rubruquis, or Ruysbroeck, the Livingstone of his age, who committed to writing a vivid account of his experiences. John of Monte Corvino ventured as far as Pekin, then known in Europe as Cambaluc and among the Mongols as Khanbaligh, "the city of the Khan."

Merciless as they were in battle, the Mongols were tolerant in religion. This was due in part to the absence among them of any well-defined system of worship. Mangu Khan, in answer to the appeals of Rubruquis, said, "We Mongols believe that there is only one God, in whom we live and die. But as God has given to the hand different fingers, so He has given to men different ways to Himself. To you Christians he has given the Holy Scriptures; to us, soothsayers and diviners."

Kublai showed the same spirit when he said to Marco Polo, "There are four prophets who are worshipped by the four different tribes on the earth. Christians look upon Christ as their God, the Saracens upon Mohammed, the Jews upon Moses, and the heathen upon Sogomombar-Khan (Buddha). I esteem and honor all four and pray that He who is supreme amongst them may lend me His help." Alexander Severus perhaps did no better when he placed side by side statues of Abraham, Christ, and Orpheus and other pagan gods. It was not till after the contact of the missionaries with the Mongols that the khans of the East adopted Buddhism, while the tribes of Persia and the West chose the rites of Islam.

In 1245 Innocent IV. despatched four Dominicans to the Mongol chief in Persia and three Franciscans to the Great Khan himself. The next effort was due to Louis IX., then engaged in his first Crusade. Ambassadors from the Mongol chief of Tartary visited the French king at Cyprus.(23) Louis returned the compliment by sending back two Dominicans in 1248, and, two years later, two Franciscans, and, in the pious hope of seeing the Tartars converted, he also sent a present of a tent embroidered with representations of Scriptural scenes and so constructed as to have the shape, when put up, of a chapel. It is from one of these two Franciscans, Rubruquis, that our first reliable information of the Mongols is drawn. He found Nestorian priests using the Syriac liturgy, which they did not understand, and joining with the Mohammedans and Buddhists in offering a blessing over the khan’s cups. Rubruquis reached Karkorum and had a hospitable reception at the court of Mangu Khan. One of Mangu’s secretaries was a Christian, another a Mohammedan, the third a Buddhist. A religious disputation was held in the khan’s presence. After Rubruquis had asserted that all God’s commandments are contained in the Scriptures, he was asked whether he thought Mangu kept them. The missionary adroitly replied that "it was his desire to lay before the khan all God’s commandments and then the khan would be able to judge for himself whether he kept them or not."

The Mongolian chiefs in Persia and the Christians were joint enemies of the Caliph of Egypt, and after the Mongolian conquest of the caliphate of Bagdad, embassies were sent by the pope to Persia, and Dominican and Franciscan convents established in that land; but after their adoption of Islam in the fourteenth century, the Mongols persecuted the Christians and the convents were destroyed.

In Central Asia among the Jagatai Mongols events took the same course. At first, 1340, permission was granted to the missionaries to prosecute their work. John of Marignola preached and baptized converts. These Mongols afterwards also adopted Mohammedanism and persecuted the Christians.

In the Mongol empire of China the efforts gave larger promise of fruitfulness. Nicolo and Maffei Polo(24) carried a request from Kublai Khan to Gregory X. for missionaries to instruct his people in Christianity and European habits. Two Dominicans accompanied the Polos on their return journey, Marco Polo being of the party. The missionaries did not reach their destination. Three years later Franciscans were sent. John of Monte Corvino, a Franciscan sent out by Nicholas IV., reached the court of the Great Khan at Cambaluc, and in 1303 was joined by Arnold, a Franciscan from Cologne. They translated the New Testament and the Psalms into the Tartar language, bought and trained one hundred and fifty boys, built two churches, one of them close to the palace and overtopping it, and baptized six thousand converts. In 1307 John was made archbishop of Pekin, archiepiscopus Cambalensis, and died 1330. The khans passed over to the Buddhist faith and in 1368 the Ming dynasty which raised itself to power abolished Christianity. It remained for the Jesuits three hundred years later to renew missionary operations in China.

§ 77. The Jews.

Would that it might be said of the mediaeval church that it felt in the well-being of the Jews, the children of Abraham according to the flesh, a tithe of the interest it manifested in the recovery of the holy places of their ancient land. But this cannot be said. Though popes, bishops, and princes, here and there, were inclined to treat them in the spirit of humanity, the predominant sentiment of Europe was the sentiment of hatred and disdain. The very nations which were draining their energies to send forth armaments to reconquer the Holy Sepulchre joined in persecuting the Jews.

Some explanation is afforded by the conduct of the Jews themselves. Their successful and often unscrupulous money dealings, the flaunting of their wealth, their exclusive social tendencies, their racial haughtiness, and their secretiveness, strained the forbearance of the Christian public to the utmost.(25) The edicts of councils and civil edicts put it beyond reasonable question that, in an offensive way, they showed contempt for the rites and symbols of the Christian faith. The provocation was great, but it does not justify a treatment of the Jewish people in all parts from Bohemia to the Atlantic which lacked the elements of common humanity. The active efforts that were made for their conversion seem to betray fully as much of the spirit of churchly arrogance as of the spirit of Christian charity. Peter the Venerable, in the prologue to his tract addressed to the Jews, said, "Out of the whole ancient world, you alone were not ignorant of Christ; yea, all peoples have listened, and you alone do not hear. Every language has confessed him, and you alone deny. Others see him, hear him, apprehend him, and you alone remain blind, and deaf, and stony of heart."

The grounds upon which the Jews were persecuted were three: 1. Their fathers had crucified Christ, and the race, predestined to bear the guilt and the punishment of the deed, was receiving its merited portion; 2. They perpetrated horrible atrocities upon Christian children, and mocked the host and the cross; 3. They imposed upon the Christians by exacting exorbitant rates of interest. In no Christian state were they safe. They were aliens in all, and had the rights of citizenship in none. The "enemies of Christ" and "the perfidious" were common names for them, and canonists and theologians use the latter expression. The ritual of Good Friday contained the words, "Let us pray also for the perfidious Jews."(26) The Decretals of Gratian, the Third and Fourth Lateran and other councils class together under one and the same canon the Jews and the Saracens.(27) Such eminent men as Peter the Venerable have more good to say of the Saracen than of the Jew.

Three classes are to be taken into account in following the treatment of the Jews,—the popes, including the prelates, the princes, and the mass of the people with their priests.

Taking the popes one by one, their utterances were, upon the whole, opposed to inhumane measures and uniformly against the forced baptism of the Jews. Gregory the Great protected them against frenzied persecution in Southern Italy. Innocent IV., 1247, denied the charge of child murder brought against them, and threatened with excommunication Christians oppressing them.(28) Martin IV., in 1419, issued a bull in which he declared that he was following his predecessors in commanding that they be not interrupted in their synagogal worship, or compelled to accept baptism, or persecuted for commercial transactions with Christians. On the other hand, the example of Innocent III. gave countenance to the severest measures, and Eugenius IV. quickly annulled the injunctions of his predecessor, Martin IV.

As for the princes, the Jews were regarded as being under their peculiar jurisdiction. At will, they levied taxes upon them, confiscated their goods, and expelled them from their realms. It was to the interest of princes to retain them as sources of revenue, and for this reason they were inclined to protect them against the violence of blind popular prejudice and rage. Frederick II. imposed upon them perpetual slavery as a vengeance upon them for the crucifixion.(29)

The inception of the Crusades was accompanied by violent outbursts against the Jews. Innocent III., in 1216, established the permanent legal basis of their persecution. Their expulsion from Spain, in 1492, represents the culminating act in the mediaeval drama of their sufferings. England, Germany, France, Spain, Portugal, and Hungary joined in their persecution. In Italy they suffered least. Tens of thousands were burned or otherwise put to death. They were driven, at one time or another, from almost every country. The alternative of baptism or death was often presented to them. The number of those who submitted to death was probably larger than the number who accepted baptism. Most of those, however, who accepted baptism afterwards openly returned to the faith of their fathers or practised its rites in secret.(30)

It is an interesting fact that, during these centuries of persecution, the Jews, especially in Spain and France, developed an energetic literary activity. Gerschom, Raschi, and the Kimchis belong to France. The names of Maimonides and Benjamin of Tudela head a long list of scholarly Spanish Jews. The pages of Graetz are filled with the names and achievements of distinguished students in medicine and other departments of study.(31)

The path of anti-Semitism was early struck by Church and Christian state. The mediaeval legislation followed closely the precedent of earlier enactments.(32) The synod of Elvira, 306, forbade Christians to eat with Jews and intermarry with them. Theodosius II., 439, excluded them from holding public office. The civil edicts, offering the alternative of baptism or death, were inaugurated by King Sisibut of Spain. When princes, as in Lyons, protected Jewish merchants, prelates violently protested, as did Agobard, archbishop of Lyons, apostle as he was in some particulars of modern enlightenment.(33) Among the enactments of this period are the following: The Jews were forbidden to employ Christian nurses, servants, or laborers, to publicly sell meat, to work on Sundays or feast days, to employ Christian physicians,(34) or to practise usury, and were commanded to make a money payment to the priest at Easter, and to wear a distinguishing patch or other object on their garments. On the other hand, Christians were forbidden to attend Jewish funerals and marriages, and were punished for borrowing from Jews.

None of the regulations was so humiliating as the one requiring the Jew to wear a distinguishing costume or a distinguishing patch upon his garments. This patch was ordered placed on the chest, or on both chest and back, so that the wearer might be distinguished from afar, as of old the leper was known by his cry "unclean," and that Christians might be prevented from ignorantly having carnal connection with the despised people. At the instance of Stephen Langton the synod of Oxford, 1222, prescribed a woollen patch, and Edward I., 1275, ordered the yellow patch worn by all over seven. Louis IX. ordered that the color of the patch should be red or saffron, the king of England that it should be yellow. Its size and shape were matters of minute enactment. The Fourth Lateran gave the weight of its great authority to this regulation about dress, and decreed that it should be enforced everywhere. Dr. Graetz pronounces this law the culminating blow in the humiliation of his kinsmen. He declares that Innocent III. brought more misery upon the Jews than all their enemies had done before, and charges him with being the first pope who turned the inhuman severity of the Church against them.(35)

The position Innocent took was that God intended the Jews to be kept, like Cain, the murderer, to wander about on the earth designed by their guilt for slavery till the time should come in the last days for their conversion.(36)

With this view, the theologians coincided. Peter the Venerable, a half-century before Innocent, presented the case in the same aspect as did the great pope, and launched a fearful denunciation against the Jews. In a letter to Louis VII. of France, he exclaimed, "What would it profit to fight against enemies of the cross in remote lands, while the wicked Jews, who blaspheme Christ, and who are much worse than the Saracens, go free and unpunished. Much more are the Jews to be execrated and hated than the Saracens; for the latter accept the birth from the Virgin, but the Jews deny it, and blaspheme that doctrine and all Christian mysteries. God does not want them to be wholly exterminated, but to be kept, like the fratricide Cain, for still more severe torment and disgrace. In this way God’s most just severity has dealt with the Jews from the time of Christ’s passion, and will continue to deal with them to the end of the world, for they are accursed, and deserve to be."(37) He counselled that they be spoiled of their ill-gotten gains and the money derived from their spoliation be applied to wrest the holy places from the Saracens.

Of a different mind was Bernard. When the preparations were being made for the Second Crusade, and the monk Radulf went up and down the Rhine, inflaming the people against the Jews, the abbot of Clairvaux set himself against the "demagogue," as Neander called Radulf.(38) He wrote a burning epistle to the archbishop of Mainz, reminding him that the Lord is gracious towards him who returns good for evil. "Does not the Church," he exclaimed, "triumph more fully over the Jews by convincing and converting them from day to day than if she once and for all should slay them by the edge of the sword!" How bitter the prejudice was is seen in the fact that when Bernard met Radulf face to face, it required all his reputation for sanctity to allay the turbulence at Mainz.(39)

Turning to England we find William of Newburgh, Roger de Hoveden, and other chroniclers. approving the Jewish persecutions. Richard of Devizes(40) speaks of "sacrificing the Jews to their father, the devil," and of sending "the bloodsuckers with blood to hell." Matthew Paris, in some of his references, seems not to have been in full sympathy with the popular animosity.

Among great English ecclesiastics the Jews had at least two friendly advocates in Hugh of Lincoln and Robert Grosseteste. Grosseteste laid down the principle that the Jews were not to be exterminated, on the grounds that the law had been given through them, and that, after passing through their second captivity, they would ultimately, in accordance with the eleventh chapter of Romans, embrace Christianity. He, however, declared that Cain was the type of the Jews, as Abel was the type of Christ. For the sake of God’s mercy, they should be preserved, that Christ might be glorified; but for the sake of God’s justice, they were to be held in captivity by the princes, that they might fulfil the prediction concerning Cain, and be vagabonds and wanderers on the earth. They should be forcibly prevented from pursuing the occupation of usurers.(41) The bishop was writing to the dowager countess of Winchester, who had offered a refuge on her lands to the Jews expelled by Simon de Montfort from Leicester. That he was not altogether above the prejudices of his age is vouched for by a letter, also written in 1244, in which he calls upon his archdeacons to prevent Jews and Christians living side by side. Grosseteste’s predecessor, Hugh of Lincoln, protected the Jews when they were being plundered and massacred in 1190, and Jews showed their respect by attending his funeral.(42)

No charge was too serious to be laid at the door of the Jews. When the Black Death swept through Europe in 1348, it did not occur to any one to think of the Saracens as the authors of that pestilence. The Jew was guilty. In Southern France and Spain, so the wild rumor ran, he had concocted poisons which were sent out wholesale and used for contaminating fountains. From Barcelona and Seville to the cities in Switzerland and Germany the unfortunate people had to suffer persecution for the alleged crime. In Strassburg, 1349, the entire Hebrew population of two thousand was seized, and as many as did not consent to baptism, were burnt in their own graveyard and their goods confiscated. In Erfurt and other places the entire Jewish population was removed by fire or expulsion.

The canonical regulations against usury gave easy excuse for declaring debts to the Jews not binding. Condemned by Tertullian and Cyprian, usury was at first forbidden to laymen as well as clerics, as by the synod of Elvira; but at the council of Nice, 325, the prohibition was restricted to the clergy. Later Jerome, Augustine, and Leo I. again applied the prohibition to all Christians. Gratian received it into the canon law. Few subjects claimed so generally the attention of the mediaeval synods as usury.(43) Alexander III., at the Third Lateran, 1179, went so far as to declare usury forbidden by the Old Testament as well as by the New Testament. Clement V. put the capstone on this sort of legislation by declaring, at the council of Vienne, 1311, null and void all state and municipal laws allowing usury and pronouncing it heresy to deny that usury is sin. No distinction was made between rates of interest. All interest was usurious. The wonder is that, with such legislation on the Church’s statute-books, any borrower should have felt bound by a debt to a Jew.

Eugenius III. offered all enlisting in the Second Crusade exemption from interest due Jewish creditors. Gregory IX. made the same offer to later Crusaders.

The charge was frequently repeated against the Jews that they were guilty of the murder of Christian children for ritualistic purposes, especially at the time of the Passover. This almost incredible crime again and again stirred the Christian population into a frenzy of excitement which issued in some of the direst miseries the Jewish people were called upon to endure.(44)

In France, Philip Augustus, using as a pretext the alleged crucifixion of a Christian child, in 1182, expelled the Jews from his realm and confiscated their goods. The decree of expulsion was repeated by Louis IX. in the year before he set out on his last crusade, by Philip the Fair in 1306 and 1311, and by other French monarchs, but it was never so strictly enforced as in Spain. Louis IX. also ordered all copies of the Targum destroyed. In 1239 Gregory IX. issued a letter to the archbishops of France, Castile, Aragon, Portugal, and England, commanding the same thing.(45)

In Germany, from the First Crusade on, the Jews were subjected to constant outbreaks, but usually enjoyed the protection of the emperors against popular fury. In the fifteenth century, they were expelled from Saxony 1432, Spires and Zürich 1435, Mainz 1438, and other localities.

In England the so-called Jewries of London, Lincoln, Oxford, and three or four other cities represented special tribunals and modes of organization, with which the usual courts of the land had nothing to do.(46) From the reign of Henry II., 1133–1189, when the detailed statements of Jewish life in England begin, bishops, priests, and convents were ready to borrow from the Jews. Nine Cistercian convents were mortgaged to the famous Aaron of Lincoln, who died 1187. He boasted that his money had built St. Albans, a boast which Freeman uses to prove the intolerable arrogance of the Jews. The arm of St. Oswald of Peterboro was held by a Jew in pawn. The usual interest charged was two pence a week on the pound, or forty-three per cent a year. And it went as high as eighty per cent. The promissory note is preserved which Herbert, pastor of Wissenden, gave to Aaron of Lincoln for 120 marks at two pence a week.(47) The Jews were tallaged by the king at pleasure. They belonged to him, as did the forests.(48) The frequency and exorbitance of the exactions under John and Henry III. are notorious. At the time of the levy of 1210 many left the kingdom. It was at that time that the famous case occurred of the Jew of Bristol, already referred to, whose teeth John ordered pulled out, one each day, till he should make over to the royal treasury ten thousand marks. The description that Matthew Paris gives is highly interesting, but it was not till four centuries had elapsed, that another historian, Thomas Fuller, commenting upon this piece of mediaeval dentistry, had the hardihood to say, this Jew, "yielding sooner, had saved his teeth, or, stubborn longer, had spared his money; now having both his purse and his jaw empty by the bargain. Condemn we here man’s cruelty, and admire Heaven’s justice; for all these sums extorted from the Jews by temporal kings axe but paying their arrearages to God for a debt they can never satisfy; namely, the crucifying of Christ." Old prejudices die hard.

Henry III.’s exactions became so intolerable that in 1255 the Jews begged to be allowed to leave the realm. This request, to rely again upon Matthew Paris, the king refused, and then, like "another Titus or Vespasian," farmed them out to his rich brother Richard, Earl of Cornwall, that, "as he himself had excoriated them, so Richard might eviscerate them."(49)

The English Crusaders, starting on the Third Crusade, freely pillaged the Jews, indignant, as the chroniclers relate, that they should have abundance and to spare while they, who were hurrying on the long journey to Jerusalem, had not enough for their barest wants.(50) It was at this time, on the evening of the coronation of Richard I., that the horrible massacre occurred in which neither sex nor age was spared. At York, five hundred were shut up in the castle, and the men, in despair, after putting to death their own wives and daughters, were many of them burned to death.(51)

English communities were roused to a lamentable pitch of excitement by the alleged crucifixion of Christian boys. Among the more notorious cases were William of Norwich 1144, Harold of Gloucester 1168, Robert of Edmonsbury 1181, and Hugh of Lincoln 1255. Although these children were popularly known as saints, none of them have been canonized by the Church. The alleged enormities perpetrated upon Hugh of Lincoln, as given by Matthew Paris, are too shocking to be enumerated at length. The same chronicler interjects the statement that the deed was "said often to have occurred." In the excitement over little Hugh, eighteen Jews were gibbeted.(52) The marvel is that the atrocious charge was believed, and that no protest against the belief has come down to us from those days.

Some English Jews, under pressure of fear, submitted to baptism, and some also of their free will. The first case of the latter kind, so far as I know, is given by Anselm.(53) The convert became a monk. An isolated case occurred here and there of a Christian turning Jew. A deacon was hanged for this offence.(54)

The last act in the history of the Jews in mediaeval England was their banishment by Edward I. in 1290. From that time until the Caroline age, England was free from Jewish inhabitants. Cromwell added to his fame by giving them protection in London.

The treatment the Jews received in Spain is justly regarded as the most merciless the race received in the Middle Ages. Edward I. protected against plunder the sixteen thousand Jews whom he banished from England. But Ferdinand of Spain, when he issued the fell decree for his Jewish subjects to leave Spain, apparently looked on without a sign of pity. Spain, through its Church councils, had been the leader in restrictive legislation. The introduction of the Inquisition made the life of this people more and more severe, although primarily its pitiless regulations had no application to them. Persecutions filled the land with ungenuine proselytes, the conversos, and these became subject to the inquisitorial court.

The final blow given by Ferdinand and Isabella fell in 1492, the year of the discovery of the New World, in a part of which was to be put into practice religious toleration as it was never before practised on the earth. The edict expelled all unbaptized Jews from Spain. Religious motives were behind it, and religious agents executed it. The immediate occasion was the panic aroused by the alleged crucifixion of the child of La Guardia—el santo niño de la Guardia—one of the most notorious cases of alleged child murder by the Jews.(55) Lope de Vega and other Spanish writers have made the case famous in Spanish literature. Ferdinand, according to Llorente, moved by the appeals of a Jewish embassy and Spanish grandees, was about to modify his sentence, when Torquemada, hastening into the presence of the king and his consort, presented the crucifix, exclaiming, "Judas Iscariot sold Christ for thirty pieces of silver. Your majesties are about to sell him for three thousand ducats. Here he is, take him and sell him."

The number of Jews who emigrated from Spain, in the summer of 1492, is estimated at 170,000 to 400, 000.(56) They went to Italy, Morocco, and the East, and, invited by king Manuel, 100,000 passed into Portugal. But here their tarrying was destined to be short. In 1495 an edict offered them the old alternative of baptism or death, and children under fourteen were taken forcibly from their parents, and the sacred Christian rite was administered to them. Ten years later two thousand of the alleged ungenuine converts were massacred in cold blood.

Such was the drama of sufferings through which the Jews were made to pass during the mediaeval period in Western Europe. As against this treatment, what efforts were made to win the Jews by appeals to the gospel? But the question might well be asked whether any appeals could be expected to win them when such a spirit of persecution prevailed. How could love and such hostility go together? The attempts to convince them were made chiefly through tracts and disputations. Anselm, while he did not direct his treatise on the atonement, cur deus homo, to the Jews, says, that his argument was sufficient to persuade both Jew and pagan. Grosseteste sought to show the fulfilment of the old law and to prove the divinity of Christ in his de cessatione legalium, written in 1231.(57) The most famous of these tracts was written by Peter the Venerable. In Migne’s edition it fills more than one hundred and forty columns, and would make a modern book of more than three hundred pages of the ordinary size. Its heading, little adapted to win the favor of the people to whom it was addressed, ran "A Tract against the Inveterate Hardness of the Jews" (inveteratam duritiem). The author proceeded to show from the Hebrew Scriptures the divinity of Christ, at the same time declaring that "to the blind even the light is as night and the sun as the shades of darkness."

Some idea can be gotten of the nature of some of Peter’s arguments from one of the many Scripture texts adduced to prove that Christ is the Son of God, Isa. lxvi. 9: "Shall I bring to the birth, and not cause to bring forth? saith Jehovah. Shall I that caused to bring forth shut the womb? saith thy God." "What could be more clear, O Jews," adds the author, "in proving the generation of the Son of God? For if God begat, so far as He begat, He is necessarily Father, and the Son of God, so far as He is begotten, is necessarily Son." In taking up the proof that the Messiah has already come, Peter naïvely says that "if the Jew shall presume to think when the argument is finished that he lives, Peter holds the sword of Goliath, and, standing over the Jew’s prostrate form, will use the weapon for his destruction, and ’with its edge’ cleave his blasphemous head in twain."(58)

If the mild abbot of Cluny, Peter the Venerable, approached the Jews in such an arrogant tone, what was to be expected from other writers, like Peter of Blois who wrote upon the Perfidy of the Jews?

Public disputations were resorted to in Southwestern Europe. Not a few Jews, "learned men, physicians, authors, and poets," to use the language of Graetz,(59) adopted the Christian faith from conviction, and "became as eager in proselyting as though they had been born Dominicans." At the public disputations, representative rabbis and chosen Christian controversialists disputed. Jewish proselytes often represented the Christian side. The most famous of these disputations, the disputation of Tortosa, extended through a year and nine months, 1413–1414, and held sixty-eight sittings. Many baptisms are reported to have followed this trial of argumentative strength, and Benedict XIII. announced his conclusions in a bull forbidding forced baptism, as opposed to the canons of the church, but insisting on the Jews wearing the distinctive patch, and enacting that they should listen to three Christian sermons every year,—on Easter, in Advent, and in midsummer. Raymundus Lullus appealed for the establishment of chairs in Hebrew with an eye to the conversion of the Jews, as did also the Dominican Raymundus of Peñaforte. At the beginning of the fifteenth century the propaganda of the eloquent preacher Vincent Ferrer was crowned with success, and the lowest estimates place the number who received baptism under his influence at twenty thousand. The most distinguished of the Spanish converts was Rabbi Solomon Helevi, 1353–1435, who occupied the archiepiscopal chair of Burgos. The Christian scholar Nicolas of Cusa, if not born a Jew, was of Jewish descent.

In London there was an attempt to reach the Jews by a sort of university settlement, the domus conversorum, intended for the protection of Jewish proselytes. It was established in 1233, and an annual grant of seven hundred marks from the royal exchequer promised for its maintenance; but no reports have come down to us of its usefulness.

These efforts relieve, it is true, the dark picture, but relieve it only a little. The racial exclusiveness of the Jew, and the defiant pride which Christendom associates with him when he attains to prosperity, still render it difficult to make any impression upon him by the presentation of the arguments for Christianity. There have been converts. Neander was a Jew born. So were Paulus Cassel and Adolf Saphir. Delitzsch had a Jew for one of his parents. Döllinger is authority for the statement that thirty years ago there were two thousand Christians in Berlin of Jewish descent. There is fortunately no feeling to-day, at least in the church of the West, that it should come to the aid of Providence in executing vengeance for the crucifixion of Christ, a thought which ruled the Christian mind in the Middle Ages. In view of the experience of the medieval church, if for no other reason, the mode of treatment suggested to the modern church is by the spirit of brotherly confidence and Christian love.
