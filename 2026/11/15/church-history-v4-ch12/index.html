§ 131. 保罗派

I. 西西里的彼得 (Petrus Siculus，约870年任驻亚美尼亚的帝国专使)：《称为保罗派的摩尼教徒史》(Historia Manichaeorum, qui Pauliciani dicuntur；Ἱστορία περὶ τῆς κενῆς καὶ ματαίας αἱρέσεως τῶν Μανιχχαίων τῶν καὶ Παυλικιανῶν λεγομένων*)。希腊文、拉丁文版，马特·拉德鲁斯 (Matth. Raderus) 编。因戈尔施塔特 (Ingolst.)，1604年。由 J. C. L. 吉泽勒 (J. C. L. Gieseler) 重新编订。哥廷根 (Göttingen)，1846年；1849年另附补编。佛提乌斯 (Photius，卒于891年)：《驳新摩尼教徒》(Adv. recentiors Manichaeos, lib. IV)，共IV卷。J. 克尔·沃尔夫 (J. Chr. Wolf) 编。汉堡 (Hamburg)，1722年；亦见加兰迪 (Gallandii)《教父文库》(Bibl. PP.)，第XIII卷，第603页及下页，以及米涅 (Migne) 编《佛提乌斯著作集》(Opera)，第II卷，第9–264栏（沃尔夫版的重印本）。关于公元870年以后该派的历史，我们必须依靠拜占庭史家君士坦丁·波菲罗根尼图斯 (Constantine Porphyrogenitus) 和凯德雷努斯 (Cedrenus)。

  1. 莫斯海姆 (Mosheim)：第IX世纪，第V章。施勒克 (Schroeckh)：第XX卷，第365页及后；第XXIII卷，第318页及后。吉本 (Gibbon)：第LIV章（第V卷，第534–554页）。F. 施密特 (F. Schmidt)：《东方保罗派史》(Historia Paulicianorum Orientalium)，哥本哈根 (Kopenhagen)，1826年。吉泽勒：《保罗派历史研究》(Untersuchungen über die Gesch. der Paulicianer)，载《研究与评论》(Studien und Kritiken)，1829年，第I期，第79页及后；及其《教会史》(Church History)，第II卷，第21页及后、第231页及后（德文版第II卷第1分册，第13、400页）。尼安德 (Neander)，第III卷，第244–270、586–592页。鲍尔 (Baur)：《中世纪基督教会》(Christl. K. im Mittelalter)，第22–25页。赫尔根勒特 (Hergenröther)，第I卷，第524–527页。哈德威克 (Hardwick)：《中世纪》(Middle Age)，第78–84页。罗伯逊 (Robertson)，第II卷，第164–173页（修订版第IV卷，第117–127页）。C. 施密特 (C. Schmidt)，载赫尔措格 (Herzog) 第2版，第XI卷，第343–348页。A. 隆巴尔 (A. Lombard)：《东方与西方的保罗派、保加尔派及善人派》(Pauliciens, Bulgares et Bons-hommes en Orient et Occident)，日内瓦 (Genève)，1879年。

基督一志论者 (Monothelites)、嗣子派 (Adoptionists)、预定派 (Predestinarians) 和贝伦加尔派 (Berengarians) 的活动都在大公教会范围之内，只在一项教义上与教会有异议，并且与前章所述大公正统教义的发展交织在一起。然而，也有一些激进的异端宗派，把基督教与异教观念混杂起来，否认与历史上的教会有任何联系，并自立为与之对抗的团体。他们在本质上是二元论的，类似古代的诺斯替派 (Gnostics) 和摩尼派 (Manichaeans)，因此，大公教会中的反对者便用一个既方便又为人憎恶的名称——新摩尼教徒 (New Manichaeans)——称呼他们；不过，保罗派 (Paulicians) 的体系与马吉安 (Marcion) 的体系更为接近。他们首先出现在东方，后来循着不为人知的途径传至西方。十三世纪，他们达到鼎盛，随后遭到教皇英诺森三世 (Innocent III) 主导的十字军征讨，受到摧毁，却未被灭绝。

这些宗派常被错误地描绘为1新教的先驱；他们只有在纯粹否定的意义上才算如此，至于他们正面主张的见解，与福音派信仰的距离，和与希腊及罗马教会信条的距离一样遥远。宗教改革出自中世纪大公教会的怀抱，保留了其普世性的教义，并维持了历史的连续性。

保罗派2是我们这一时期最重要的宗派。他们的活动仅限于东方教会的地域。他们在亚美尼亚兴盛起来；在那里，基督教与波斯教 (Parsism) 发生冲突，并掺入了二元论思想。他们很可能继承了摩尼派和马吉安派 (Marcionites) 的某些传统。

I. 他们的希腊反对者3认为，该派的名称源于保罗和约翰两兄弟；他们是撒摩撒他 (Samosata) 一位名叫卡利尼刻 (Kallinike) 的摩尼教妇人的儿子。然而，现代史家4认为，这一名称源于他们对圣保罗的偏爱，他们把他置于众使徒之首；这种解释更为可信。他们从保罗的门徒中借用名字，作为主要教师的称号（西拉、提多、提摩太、推基古、以巴弗提），又以保罗各会众的名称命名自己的会众（哥林多、腓立比、亚该亚等）。他们自己则宁愿只称为“基督徒”（Χριστιανοί*、Χριστοπολῖται*），以区别于罗马国教的信奉者（Ῥωμαίους*）。

  1. 该派的创始人是叙利亚人君士坦丁 (Constantine)，出身于撒摩撒他附近马纳纳利斯 (Mananalis) 的一个诺斯替派（马吉安派）会众。他受圣保罗书信的启发，自称是保罗真正的门徒，以西拉为名，在亚美尼亚的基博萨 (Kibossa)，以及本都 (Pontus) 和加帕多家 (Cappadocia) 一带传播二元论教义，二十七年间大获成功；直到皇帝君士坦丁·波戈纳图斯 (Constantine Pogonatus，668–685年) 派遣军官西缅 (Symeon) 前来逮捕并处决他。684年，他被石头打死，会众也被驱散。然而，西缅被君士坦丁—西拉从容无畏的勇气所打动，因而归信，重新召集了会众，并以提多为名领导他们。查士丁尼二世 (Justinian II) 得知此事后，依据惩治摩尼派的法律，判处他和其他领袖火刑（690年）。

然而，尽管屡遭逼迫，并有内部分裂，该派仍传遍小亚细亚 (Asia Minor)。当其衰落时，一位热忱的改革者塞尔吉乌斯 (Sergius)，又名推基乌斯 (Tychieus)，兴起成为该派的第二位创始人（801–835年）。他因一位妇人而归信，走访旧有的会众，建立新的会众，传道并撰写书信，反对号称“污秽者”（ὁ ῥυπαρός*）的巴阿尼斯 (Baanes) 所行的反律法主义，并施行严格的纪律。他的追随者被称为塞尔吉乌斯派 (Sergiotes)，以区别于巴阿尼斯派 (Baanites)。

该派的命运随希腊皇帝的政策而变化。反对圣像的伊苏里亚人皇帝利奥 (Leo the Isaurian) 未曾侵扰他们；该派领袖格格奈修斯 (Gegnaesius) 接受牧首审查、结果令其满意之后，利奥还赐给他一封保护信，使其免遭逼迫；不过，这位狡猾的异端分子回答问题的方式蒙蔽了牧首。亚美尼亚人利奥 (Leo the Armenian，813–820年) 组织远征，要使他们归信，赦免背弃该派的人，处死坚持不改的人。恢复圣像崇拜的狄奥多拉 (Theodora) 残酷地逼迫他们；在她短暂的统治期间，有十万保罗派 (Paulicians) 信徒死于刀剑、绞架或烈火（844年）。如此庞大的数目中，或许包括了许多毁像派 (iconoclasts) 人士。

这些暴行激怒了保罗派 (Paulicians)，他们在卡尔贝阿斯 (Karbeas) 领导下举起反叛的旗帜。他率五千人逃往撒拉逊人 (Saracens) 那里，在阿拉伯边境建立了坚固的堡垒特弗里卡 (Tephrica)5，并与穆斯林结盟，多次成功地进犯拜占庭领土。他的女婿克里索刻雷斯 (Chrysocheres) 一路推进到以弗所 (Ephesus)，把主教座堂变成马厩（867年），但于871年被希腊人杀死，该派只得臣服于马其顿人巴西尔皇帝 (Basil the Macedonian)。巴西尔派修士西西里的彼得 (Petrus Siculus) 到他们中间；彼得由此得以了解他们的教义，并为自己的著作搜集了材料。

此后，该派失去了政治上的重要性，逐渐从历史中消失。约970年，许多人被迁往色雷斯 (Thrace) 的腓立波波利斯 (Philippopolis)，充任边境守卫，并获准保有信仰。阿历克修斯·科穆宁 (Alexius Comnenus，1081–1118年) 与他们的领袖辩论，奖赏改宗者，惩罚顽固不化者。1204年，十字军攻占君士坦丁堡 (Constantinople) 时，还发现了该派的一些残余。

  1. 我们对保罗派教义和实践的了解，仅来自正统派反对者的记述，以及塞尔吉乌斯书信的少量残篇。这些教义和实践是二元论 (dualism)、造物主说 (demiurgism)、幻影说 (docetism)、神秘主义 (mysticism) 和伪保罗主义 (pseudo-Paulinism) 的奇异混合，在许多方面类似马吉安的诺斯替体系。

  1. 二元论是他们的基本原则。6善神创造了属灵世界；恶神，即造物主 (demiurge)，创造了感官世界。保罗派，即真正的基督徒，敬拜前者；“罗马人”，即大公教会信徒，敬拜后者。

  2. 蔑视物质。身体是邪欲的居所，本身就是不洁的。它把神圣的灵魂囚禁其中，如同关在监牢里。

  3. 幻影说。基督以轻灵的身体从天降下，经过马利亚的腹中，如同经过一条通道；他的受苦只是表象，并非真实，由此开始了将灵从物质锁链中救赎出来的过程。

  4. 童贞女马利亚并非“上帝之母”，她与耶稣的关系纯属外在。西西里的彼得说，他们甚至不容许她列身于善良而有德行的妇女之中。真正的“上帝之母”(theotokos) 是天上的耶路撒冷；基督从那里出来，也回到那里去。

  5. 他们拒绝《旧约》，视之为造物主的作品，也拒绝彼得的书信。他们认为彼得是假使徒，因为他否认自己的主，所传的是犹太教而非基督教，是保罗的仇敌（加2:11），又是大公教会教阶制度的支柱。他们接受四福音书、《使徒行传》、保罗的十四封书信，以及雅各、约翰和犹大的书信。然而，在后来的时期，他们似乎像马吉安一样，只限于采用保罗和路加的著作，可能还加上《约翰福音》。他们也声称拥有一封《致老底嘉人书》；不过，这很可能就是《以弗所书》。他们采用寓意解经法。

  6. 他们拒绝祭司制度、圣礼、对圣徒和圣物的崇拜、画十字的记号（重病时除外），以及宗教中一切外在形式。洗礼仅指圣灵的洗；与基督的身体和宝血相交，仅指与他的话语和教义相交。

保罗派没有祭司（ἱερεῖς* 和 πρεσβύτεροι*），取而代之的是教师和牧者（διδάσκαλοι* 和 ποιμένες*）、同工或巡回传教士（συνέκδημοι*），以及文书（νωτάριοι*）。他们没有教堂，而有称为“祈祷所”（προσευχαί*）的聚会场所；不过，创始人和领袖被尊为“使徒”和“先知”。在他们中间，找不到摩尼教将人分为“选民”(electi) 与“信徒”(credentes) 两等的痕迹。

  1. 他们的道德观是禁欲主义的。他们力求使灵摆脱物质身体的权势，却不谴责婚姻和食肉；但巴阿尼斯派 (Baanites) 却走向相反的极端，以反律法主义 (antinomian) 滥用肉体，沉溺于淫乱，甚至乱伦。在这两个极端上，他们都类似诺斯替各派 (Gnostic sects)。据佛提乌斯 (Photius) 说，保罗派 (Paulicians) 也全然缺乏诚实，毫无顾忌地否认自己的信仰，所依据的原则是：为达到良善目的，说谎是正当的。

§ 132. 祈祷派及东方的其他宗派

I. 米迦勒·普塞洛斯 (Michael Psellus，十一世纪君士坦丁堡的一位学者)：《论鬼魔活动的对话》(Διάλογος περὶ ἐνεργείας δαιμόνων*)，戈尔曼 (Gaulmin) 编，巴黎 (Par.)，1615年；另有 J. F. 布瓦索纳德 (J. F. Boissonade) 编本，纽伦堡 (Norimbergae)，1838年。凯德雷努斯 (Cedrenus，十一世纪)：《历史纲要》(Histor. Compend.)，波恩版 (ed. Bonn.)，第I卷，第514页。——关于较早的祈祷派 (Euchites) 和弥撒利派 (Messalians)，参见伊皮法纽 (Epiphanius)《驳异端》(Haer.)，第80；狄奥多勒 (Theodoret)《教会史》(Hist. Eccl.)，第IV卷，第10章；大马士革的约翰 (John of Damascus)《论异端》(De Haer.)，第80章；佛提乌斯 (Photius)《文库》(Bibl. cod.)，第52篇；以及瓦尔希 (Walch)《异端史》(Ketzer-Historie)，第III卷，第481页及后、第536页及后。

  1. 施尼策尔 (Schnitzer)：《十一世纪的祈祷派》(Die Euchiten im elften Jahrh.)，载施蒂尔姆 (Stirm) 的《符腾堡福音派教牧研究》(Studien der evang. Geistlichkeit Würtemberg’s)，第XI卷，第I期，第169页。吉泽勒，第II卷，第232页及下页。尼安德，第III卷，第590页及后，比较第II卷，第277页及后。

祈祷派是信奉神秘主义的修士，持有源自波斯教的二元论原则。他们认为，每个人自出生起就有一个鬼魔住在里面，只有借着不住的默祷才能将它赶出；他们将这种祷告置于一切属灵操练之上。他们的名称即由此而来。7他们又因夸耀自己的狂喜体验而被民众称为狂热派 (Enthusiasts)；在这些体验中，他们自以为领受了特别的启示。普塞洛斯称他们为“魔鬼崇拜者”。他们蔑视一切外在的敬拜形式。传闻指控他们在秘密聚会中淫乱、杀婴；但早期基督徒也曾遭受同样的传言，这些传言不值得相信。

十一世纪，他们出现在美索不达米亚 (Mesopotamia) 和亚美尼亚，与保罗派有某种联系。他们很可能是四、五世纪较早的叙利亚祈祷派或弥撒利派的后继者；那些人自负已经达到禁欲生活的至高完善境界，蔑视体力劳动和一切日常职业，靠施舍为生——他们是托钵修士最早的样本。

十一世纪末，波格米勒派 (Bogomiles，祈祷派的斯拉夫语名称)8和卡塔里派 (Catharists，即纯洁派、清教徒)从祈祷派中产生，并从保加利亚 (Bulgaria) 传播到西方。在下一时期，我们还要论述他们。

东方另有一派，称为通德拉克派 (Thondracians，得名于通德拉克村 [Thondrac])，由保罗派信徒森巴特 (Sembat) 于833至854年间在亚拉腊 (Ararat) 省创立。他们出自保罗派，虽遭逼迫，仍在亚美尼亚使许多人归附，其中包括一位名叫雅各 (Jacob) 的主教。他于1002年宣讲，抨击亚美尼亚教会的腐败，却遭敌人施以烙刑、当众羞辱、监禁，最后被杀。9

关于出现在上弗里吉亚 (Upper Phrygia) 的阿辛吉派 (Athingians)，我们所知甚少。10他们似乎带有浓厚的犹太教倾向。他们遵守律法的一切礼仪，惟独以洗礼代替割礼。尼安德推测，他们是保罗所反对的歌罗西谬误派的后继者。

§ 133. 西方的新摩尼教徒

I. 关于中世纪各教派的主要史料属于下一时期，即教皇英诺森三世 (Innocent III.)、霍诺留三世 (Honorius III.)、克莱尔沃的伯尔纳 (Bernhard of Clairvaux)、可敬者彼得 (Peter the Venerable) 的书信；各次会议的议事录；编年史；以及专门反驳这些教派的著作，其中主要的是伦巴第 (Lombardy) 多明我会修士赖内里乌斯·萨科尼 (Reinerius Sacchoni，卒于1259年) 的作品，他本人曾作异端信徒达十七年。这些史料收集在《教父文献大全》(Maxima Biblioth. Patr.，里昂 [Lugd.]，1677年，第XXII、XXIV卷)；马尔泰纳 (Martene) 与迪朗 (Durand) 的《未刊文献新宝库》(Thesaurus novus anecdotorum，巴黎 [Par.]，1682年)；穆拉托里 (Muratori) 的《意大利史家集》(Rerum Italic. Scriptores，米兰 [Mediol.]，1723年及后)；布凯 (Bouquet) 的《高卢与法国史家汇编》(Recueil des historiens des Gaules et de la France，巴黎 [Par.]，1738年及后) 等书中。参见哈恩 (Hahn)，第I卷，第23页及后的文献目录。

西方的异端教派主要分为三个不同的类别：1) 二元论派或摩尼派；2) 泛神论派与神秘主义派；3) 圣经派，即瓦勒度派 (Waldenses)。他们彼此差异甚大，却一致憎恨教皇的教会与祭司制度。他们兴起的原因多种多样：异教观念与古老异端的残余；对教会和教士腐败的反抗；理性对专横权威的反叛；以及民众对上帝之道的渴求。在十二、十三世纪，他们以惊人的速度从保加利亚传播到西班牙，尤其遍布意大利和法国南部，致使处于权势巅峰的教皇制度（英诺森三世在位时）动用全部力量，以武力镇压他们。只有一派在这场十字军征伐中幸存下来，就是瓦勒度派，因为他们忠实持守圣经所明确教导的真理。

在西方，异端倾向首次以有组织的形式出现，是在十一世纪，当时教会与教皇制度的腐败已达到顶点。在那个时代的人看来，这种倾向是摩尼派异端的延续或复兴。11两者之间的联系纽带是二元论原则。古代摩尼派从未被烈火与刀剑彻底根除，而是继续秘密存在于意大利和法国，等候有利时机，从隐蔽中重现。我们也不可忽视东方的影响。保罗派常在拜占庭 (Byzantine) 军旗下，从色雷斯 (Thrace) 和保加利亚被调往意大利与西西里 (Sicily) 的希腊属地，并在那里散播其二元论、幻影说以及对居统治地位的教会的仇恨种子。12

新摩尼教徒最早于1022年在阿基坦 (Aquitania) 和奥尔良 (Orleans)、1025年在阿拉斯 (Arras)、1030年在都灵 (Turin) 附近的蒙特福尔特 (Monteforte)、1025年在戈斯拉尔 (Goslar) 被发现。他们教导上帝与物质之间的二元对立，以幻影说看待基督的人性，反对敬拜圣徒与圣像，并拒绝整个大公教会及其一切物质性的施恩媒介，代之以属灵的洗礼、属灵的圣餐，以及以按手为记号的入教仪式。有些人把基督的一生化解为每个人里面神圣生命的神话或象征。他们普遍遵守严厉的道德规范，禁绝婚姻、动物性食物和醉人的饮料。民众把苍白消瘦的面容视为异端的标志。该派的信徒是平民，但领袖中也有祭司，有时还乔装掩饰身份。其中一人丢多内 (Dieudonné)，是奥尔良教会的领唱者，死时仍是大公教会信徒；但他死后三年，人们发现他与异端有牵连，便将他的骸骨掘出，移离祝圣之地。

东方以柴堆和刀剑逼迫异议者的做法，在西方也被仿效。民众狂热的愤怒，助长了祭司的不宽容。1022年，十三名新摩尼教徒在奥尔良被判处火刑。其他地方也有类似的处决。在米兰，异端信徒只有两种选择：或向十字架下拜，或受死；但大多数人投身于火焰之中。

少数人超越了那个时代逼迫异己的精神，效法图尔的圣马丁 (St. Martin of Tours)；他曾强烈抗议在特里尔 (Treves) 处决普利西利安派 (Priscillianists)。约1047年，列日 (Liège) 主教瓦佐 (Wazo) 被征询应如何处置马恩河畔沙隆 (Châlons-sur-Marne) 教区的异端信徒时，发出了主张宽容的呼声。他说，这些教义固然必须被定为非基督教的教义；但我们有责任容忍传授这些教义的人，效法我们的救主；他温柔谦卑，来不是要争竞，而是忍受羞辱和十字架上的死。麦子与稗子的比喻教导我们，要耐心等候走入歧途的邻舍悔改。他对同任主教的人说：“我们作主教的，应当记得，在受按立时，我们所领受的不是世俗权力的刀剑，不是杀人的使命，而只是使人得生命的使命。”他们所当做的，只是将顽固的异端信徒逐出教会的团契，并保护其他人免受这些危险教义的影响。13

CHAPTER XII. HERETICAL SECTS.

§ 131. The Paulicians.

I. Petrus Siculus (imperial commissioner in Armenia, about 870): Historia Manichaeorum, qui Pauliciani dicuntur ( JIstoriva peri; th'" kenh'" kai; mataiva" aiJrevsew" tw'n Maniccaivwn tw'n kai; Paulikianw'n legomevnwn). Gr. Lat. ed. Matth. Raderus. Ingolst., 1604. Newly ed. by J. C. L. Gieseler. Göttingen, 1846, with an appendix, 1849. Photius (d. 891): Adv. recentiors Manichaeos, lib. IV. Ed. by J. Chr. Wolf. Hamburg, 1722; in Gallandii "Bibl. PP." XIII. 603 sq., and in Photii Opera ed. Migne, Tom. II., col. 9–264 (reprint of Wolf). For the history of the sect after a.d. 870 we must depend on the Byzantine historians, Constantine Porphyrogenitus and Cedrenus.

  1. Mosheim: Century IX., ch. V. Schroeckh: vols. XX. 365 sqq., and XXIII. 318 sqq. Gibbon: Ch. LIV. (vol. V. 534–554). F. Schmidt: Historia Paulicianorum Orientalium. Kopenhagen, 1826. Gieseler: Untersuchungen über die Gesch. der Paulicianer, in the "Studien und Kritiken," 1829, No. I., 79 sqq.; and his Church History, II. 21 sqq., and 231 sqq. (Germ. ed. II. 1, 13 and 400). The Monothelites, the Adoptionists, the Predestinarians, and the Berengarians moved within the limits of the Catholic church, dissented from it only in one doctrine, and are interwoven with the development of’ catholic orthodoxy which has been described in the preceding chapter. But there were also radical heretical sects which mixed Christianity with heathen notions, disowned all connection with the historic church, and set themselves up against it as rival communities. They were essentially dualistic, like the ancient Gnostics and Manichaeans, and hence their Catholic opponents called them by the convenient and hated name of New Manichaeans; though the system of the Paulicians has more affinity with that of Marcion. They appeared first in the East, and spread afterwards by unknown tracks in the West. They reached their height in the thirteenth century, when they were crushed, but not annihilated, by a crusade under Pope Innocent III.

These sects have often been falsely represented(1) as forerunners of Protestantism; they are so only in a purely negative sense, while in their positive opinions they differ as widely from the evangelical as from the Greek and Roman creed. The Reformation came out of the bosom of Mediaeval Catholicism, retained its oecumenical doctrines, and kept up the historic continuity.

The Paulicians(2) are the most important sect in our period. They were confined to the territory of the Eastern church. They flourished in Armenia, where Christianity came in conflict with Parsism and was mixed with dualistic ideas. They probably inherited some traditions of the Manichaeans and Marcionites.

I. Their name is derived by their Greek opponents(3) from two brothers, Paul and John sons of a Manichaean a woman Kallinike, in Samosata; but, more probably, by modern historians(4) from their preference for St. Paul whom they placed highest among the Apostles. They borrowed the names of their leading teachers from his disciples (Sylvanus, Titus, Timothy, Tychicus, Epaphroditus), and called their congregations after his (Corinth, Philippi, Achaia, etc.). They themselves preferred simply the name "Christians" (Cristianoiv, Cristopoli'tai), in opposition to the professors of the Roman state-religion ( JRwmaivou").

  1. The founder of the sect is Constantine a Syrian from a Gnostic (Marcionite) congregation in Mananalis near Samosata. Inspired by the epistles of St. Paul and pretending to be his genuine disciple, he propagated under the name of Sylvanus dualistic doctrines in Kibossa in Armenia and in the regions of Pontus and Cappadocia, with great success for twenty-seven years, until the Emperor Constantine Pogonatus (668–685) sent an officer, Symeon, for his arrest and execution. He was stoned to death in 684, and his congregation scattered. But Symeon was struck and converted by the serene courage of Constantine-Sylvanus, revived the congregation, and ruled it under the name of Titus. When Justinian II. heard of it, he condemned him and the other leaders to death by fire (690), according to the laws against the Manichaeans.

But in spite of repeated persecution and inner dissensions, the sect spread throughout Asia Minor. When it decayed, a zealous reformer rose in the person of Sergius, called Tychieus, the second founder of the sect (801–835). He had been converted by a woman, visited the old congregations and founded new ones, preached and wrote epistles, opposed the antinomian practices of Baanes, called "the Filthy" (oJ rJuparov"), and introduced strict discipline. His followers were called Sergiotes in distinction from the Baanites.

The fate of the sect varied with the policy of the Greek emperors. The iconoclastic Leo the Isaurian did not disturb them, and gave the leader of the sect, Gegnaesius, after a satisfactory examination by the patriarch, a letter of protection against persecution; but the wily heretic had answered the questions in a way that deceived the patriarch. Leo the Armenian (813–820) organized an expedition for their conversion, pardoning the apostates and executing the constant. Theodora, who restored the worship of images, cruelly persecuted them, and under her short reign one hundred thousand Paulicians were put to death by the sword, the gibbet, or the flames (844). Perhaps this large number included many iconoclasts.

Provoked by these cruelties, the Paulicians raised the standard of revolt under the lead of Karbeas. He fled with five thousand to the Saracens, built a strong fort, Tephrica,(5) on the Arab frontier, and in alliance with the Moslems made successful military invasions into the Byzantine territory. His son-in-law, Chrysocheres, proceeded as far as Ephesus, and turned the cathedral into a stable (867), but was killed by the Greeks in 871, and the sect had to submit to the Emperor Basil the Macedonian. He sent among them the monk Petrus Siculus, who thus became acquainted with their doctrines and collected the materials for his work.

After this the sect lost its political significance, and gradually disappeared from history. Many were transferred to Philippopolis in Thrace about 970, as guards of the frontier, and enjoyed toleration. Alexius Comnenus (1081–1118) disputed with their leaders, rewarded the converts, and punished the obstinate. The Crusaders found some remains in 1204, when they captured Constantinople.

  1. The doctrines and practices of the Paulicians are known to us only from the reports of the orthodox opponents and a few fragments of the epistles of Sergius. They were a strange mixture of dualism, demiurgism, docetism, mysticism and pseudo-Paulinism, and resemble in many respects the Gnostic system of Marcion.

  1. Dualism was their fundamental principle.(6) The good God created the spiritual world; the bad God or demiurge created the sensual world. The former is worshipped by the Paulicians, i.e. the true Christians, the latter by the "Romans" or Catholics.

  2. Contempt of matter. The body is the seat of evil desire, and is itself impure. It holds the divine soul as in a prison.

  3. Docetism. Christ descended from heaven in an ethereal body, passed through the womb of Mary as through a channel, suffered in appearance, but not in reality, and began the process of redemption of the spirit from the chains of matter.

  4. The Virgin Mary was not "the mother of God," and has a purely external connection with Jesus. Peter the Sicilian says, that they did not even allow her a place among the good and virtuous women. The true theotokos is the heavenly Jerusalem, from which Christ came out and to which he returned.

  5. They rejected the Old Testament as the work of the Demiurge, and the Epistles of Peter. They regarded Peter as a false apostle, because he denied his master, preached Judaism rather than Christianity, was the enemy of Paul (Gal. 2:11) and the pillar of the Catholic hierarchy. They accepted the four Gospels, the Acts, fourteen Epistles of Paul, and the Epistles of James, John and Jude. At a later period, however, they seem to have confined themselves, like Marcion, to the writings of Paul and Luke, adding to them probably the Gospel of John. They claimed also to possess an Epistle to the Laodiceans; but this was probably identical with the Epistle to the Ephesians. Their method of exposition was allegorical.

  6. They rejected the priesthood, the sacraments, the worship of saints and relics, the sign of the cross (except in cases of serious illness), and all externals in religion. Baptism means only the baptism of the Spirit; the communion with the body and blood of Christ is only a communion with his word and doctrine.

In the place of priests (iJerei'" and presbuvteroi) the Paulicians had teachers and pastors (didavskaloi and poimevne"), companions or itinerant missionaries (sunevkdhmoi), and scribes (nwtavrioi). In the place of churches they had meeting-houses called "oratories" (proseucaiv); but the founders and leaders were esteemed as "apostles" and "prophets." There is no trace of the Manichaean distinction between two classes of the electi and credentes.

  1. Their morals were ascetic. They aimed to emancipate the spirit from the power of the material body, without, however, condemning marriage and the eating of flesh; but the Baanites ran into the opposite extreme of an antinomian abuse of the flesh, and reveled in licentiousness, even incest. In both extremes they resembled the Gnostic sects. According to Photius, the Paulicians were also utterly deficient in veracity, and denied their faith without scruple on the principle that falsehood is justifiable for a good end.

§ 132. The Euchites and other Sects in the East.

I. Michael Psellus (a learned Constantinopolitan, 11th cent.): Diavlogo" peri; ejnergeiva" daimovnwn, ed. Gaulmin. Par. 1615; also by J. F. Boissonade. Norimbergae, 1838. Cedrenus (in the 11th cent.): Histor. Compend. (ed. Bonn. I. 514).—On the older Euchites and Messalians see Epiphanius (Haer. 80), Theodoret (Hist. Eccl. IV. 10), John of Damascus (De Haer., c. 80), Photius (Bibl. cod. 52), and Walch: Ketzer-Historie, III. 481 sqq. and 536 sqq.

  1. Schnitzer: Die Euchiten im elften Jahrh., in Stirm’s "Studien der evang. Geistlichkeit Würtemberg’s," vol. XI., H. I. 169. Gieseler, II. 232 sq. Neander, III. 590 sqq., comp. II. 277 sqq.

The Euchites were mystic monks with dualistic principles derived from Parsism. They held that a demon dwells in every man from his birth, and can be expelled only by unceasing silent prayer, which they exalted above every spiritual exercise. Hence their name.(7) They were also called Enthusiasts by the people on account of their boasted ecstasies, in which they fancied that they received special revelations. Psellus calls them "devil-worshippers." They despised all outward forms of worship. Rumor charged them with lewdness and infanticide in their secret assemblies; but the same stories were told of the early Christians, and deserve no credit.

They appear in the eleventh century in Mesopotamia and Armenia, in some connection with the Paulicians. They were probably the successors of the older Syrian Euchites or Messalians of the fourth and fifth centuries, who in their conceit had reached the height of ascetic perfection, despised manual labor and all common occupations, and lived on alms—the first specimens of mendicant friars.

From the Euchites sprang towards the close of the eleventh century the Bogomiles (the Slavonic name for Euchites),(8) and Catharists (i.e. the Purists, Puritans), and spread from Bulgaria into the West. They will occupy our attention in the next period.

Another Eastern sect, called Thondracians (from the village Thondrac), was organized by Sembat, a Paulician, in the province of Ararat, between 833 and 854. They sprang from the Paulicians, and in spite of persecution made numerous converts in Armenia, among them a bishop, Jacob, in 1002, who preached against the corruptions in the Armenian church, but was branded, exposed to public scorn, imprisoned, and at last killed by his enemies.(9)

Little is known of the sect of the Athingians who appeared in Upper Phrygia.(10) They seem to have been strongly Judaistic. They observed all the rites of the law except circumcision, for which they substituted baptism. Neander conjectures, that they were the successors of the Colossian errorists opposed by St. Paul.

§ 133. The New Manichaeans in the West.

I. The chief sources for the sects of the Middle Age belong to the next period, namely, the letters of Pope Innocent III., Honorius III., Bernhard of Clairvaux, Peter the Venerable; the acts of Councils; the chronicles; and the special writings against them, chiefly those of the Dominican monk Reinerius Sacchoni of Lombardy (d. 1259), who was himself a heretic for seventeen years. The sources are collected in the "Maxima Biblioth. Patr." (Lugd., 1677, Tom. XXII., XXIV.); in Martene and Durand’s "Thesaurus novus anecdotorum" (Par., 1682); in Muratori’s "Rerum Italic. Scriptores" (Mediol., 1723 sqq.); in Bouquet’s "Recueil des historiens des Gaules et de la France" (Par., 1738 sqq.), etc. See the Lit. in Hahn I. 23 sqq.

The heretical sects in the West are chiefly of three distinct classes: 1) the dualistic or Manichaean; 2) the pantheistic and mystic; 3) the biblical (the Waldenses). Widely differing among themselves, they were united in hatred of the papal church and the sacerdotal system. They arose from various causes: the remains of heathen notions and older heresies; opposition to the corruptions of the church and the clergy; the revolt of reason against tyrannical authority; and popular thirst for the word of God. They spread with astonishing rapidity during the twelfth and thirteenth centuries from Bulgaria to Spain, especially through Italy and Southern France, and called forth all the energies of the papacy at the zenith of its power (under Innocent III.) for their forcible suppression. One only survived the crusade, the Waldenses, owing to their faithful adherence to the positive truths of the Scriptures.

In the West the heretical tendency in organized form made its first appearance during the eleventh century, when the corruption of the church and the papacy had reached its height. It appeared to that age as a continuation or revival of the Manichaean heresy.(11) The connecting link is the dualistic principle. The old Manichaeans were never quite extirpated with fire and sword, but continued secretly in Italy and France, waiting for a favorable opportunity to emerge from obscurity. Nor must we overlook the influence from the East. Paulicians were often transported under Byzantine standards from Thrace and Bulgaria to the Greek provinces of Italy and Sicily, and spread the seed of their dualism and docetism and hatred of the ruling church.(12)

New Manichaeans were first discovered in Aquitania and Orleans, in 1022, in Arras, 1025, in Monteforte near Turin, 1030, in Goslar, 1025. They taught a dualistic antagonism between God and matter, a docetic view of the humanity of Christ, opposed the worship of saints and images, and rejected the whole Catholic church with all the material means of grace, for which they substituted a spiritual baptism, a spiritual eucharist, and a symbol of initiation by the imposition of hands. Some resolved the life of Christ into a myth or symbol of the divine life in every man. They generally observed an austere code of morals, abstained from marriage, animal food, and intoxicating drinks. A pallid, emaciated face was regarded by the people as a sign of heresy. The adherents of the sect were common people, but among their leaders were priests, sometimes in disguise. One of them, Dieudonné, precentor of the church in Orleans, died a Catholic; but when three years after his death his connection with the heretics was discovered, his bones were dug up and removed from consecrated ground.

The Oriental fashion of persecuting dissenters by the faggot and the sword was imitated in the West. The fanatical fury of the people supported the priests in their intolerance. Thirteen New Manichaeans were condemned to the stake at Orleans in 1022. Similar executions occurred in other places. At Milan the heretics were left the choice either to bow before the cross, or to die; but the majority plunged into the flames.

A few men rose above the persecuting spirit of the age, following the example of St. Martin of Tours, who had vigorously protested against the execution of the Priscillianists at Treves. Wazo, bishop of Liège, about 1047, raised his voice for toleration when he was asked for his opinion concerning the treatment of the heretics in the diocese of Châlons-sur-Marne. Such doctrines, he said, must be condemned as unchristian; but we are bound to bear with the teachers after the example of our Saviour, who was meek and humble and came not to strive, but rather to endure shame and the death of the cross. The parable of the wheat and the tares teaches us to wait patiently for the repentance of erring neighbors. "We bishops," he tells his fellow-bishops, "should remember that we did not receive, at our ordination, the sword of secular power, the vocation to slay, but only the vocation to make alive." All they had to do was to exclude obstinate heretics from the communion of the church and to guard others against their dangerous doctrines.(13)
